# B1 — referrals pay DGD. What now.

**2026-09-21.** DGD has confirmed that referrals are compensated in DGD or in
something convertible to it. This sets out the options and what each costs.

**Not legal advice.** The application of an App Store guideline to an off-app
payout is exactly the kind of question DGD's counsel should answer. What
follows is the engineering picture and an honest read of the risk, so that the
decision is made with the costs visible.

---

## The rule, and why the app is exposed

> *"Cryptocurrency apps may not offer currency for completing tasks, such as
> downloading other apps, encouraging other users to download, posting to
> social networks, etc."* — App Store Review Guidelines, 3.1.5(v)

| Element | Does it apply? |
|---|---|
| "Cryptocurrency apps" | Yes. A DGD ticker with a DGD member path. |
| "may not offer currency" | The **app** offers nothing. The **site** pays. This is the entire defence. |
| "encouraging other users to download" | Yes, and literally. |

The third row is the problem. **Display QR encodes
`https://digitalgold.co/app?ref=USERNAME`** — an app-download link carrying a
referral code that now earns DGD. The guideline's own example is "encouraging
other users to download". This is not analogous to the prohibited thing; it is
the prohibited thing, with the payout moved one hop away.

The "the app doesn't pay" argument is real but thin. The app ships the
mechanism, the code, the QR and the share sheet. Only settlement happens
elsewhere. A reviewer told that the programme pays in DGD is being asked to
accept that the app is a bystander to a system it is the front door for.

**A rejection here is not a normal rejection.** 3.1.5 sits in the section
reviewers treat as a policy matter rather than a bug, it invites scrutiny of
the rest of the submission, and a second attempt arrives with history attached.

### Google Play

Apple's clause is explicit. I could not find an equivalent explicit clause in
Play policy, and Play does host crypto apps with referral programmes — but
store listings are not policy text, and I am not willing to tell you Play is
safe on that basis. Counsel should check Play's Financial Services and
incentivised-installs policies directly before Android is treated as clear.

## Options

Ordered by how much they reduce risk, not by how much anyone will enjoy them.

### A. Remove invites from the app entirely

Invite Friends section, QR, Copy Invite Link, Copy Invite Code, the social
share buttons. Referral continues on the website, where the guideline does not
reach.

- **Engineering:** roughly half a day, both platforms. The section is
  self-contained in `SignupPreviewScreen`; `DigitalGoldSite`'s URL builders
  lose their callers. Tests and reviewer notes to update.
- **Residual risk:** lowest. The reviewer notes become trivially true again —
  the app does not reward, encourage downloads, or carry a referral code.
- **Cost:** a feature someone asked for, and the in-app viral loop.

### B. Remove the app-download QR only

Keep Copy Invite Link (`/signup?ref=`); drop Display QR (`/app?ref=`).

- **Engineering:** about two hours. Delete `InviteQRCode` usage and the
  `appInviteURL` builder.
- **Residual risk:** **partial, and I would not rely on it.** It removes the
  most literal match, but a signup referral that pays DGD still plausibly
  reads as "encouraging other users to download" once the payout is known.
- Reasonable only as a supplement to C, not as a substitute for A.

### C. DGD stops paying DGD for referrals

Pay nothing, or pay in something with no monetary value and no conversion path
— the same standing the arcade's XP already has.

- **Engineering:** none. Not a code change.
- **Residual risk:** lowest of all, and it makes the original answer "no"
  truthfully rather than by argument.
- **Cost:** entirely commercial, and DGD's call. Worth noting that if referral
  compensation is load-bearing for growth, A plus C together still leave the
  website free to run the programme — it is only the app that must be clean.

### D. Submit as-is and argue it

- **Engineering:** none now.
- **Residual risk:** highest, and asymmetric. If it lands, nothing was spent.
  If it does not, the cost is a policy-level rejection on a submission that is
  otherwise in good shape, plus rework under time pressure and a worse
  starting position for the next attempt.

## Asked 2026-09-21: could games or the quiz award DGD if they are skill-based? Failing that, "validation credits"?

Not legal advice, same caveat as above. But the first one has a clear answer
and the second has a trap in it.

### Skill does not rescue it

The guideline, verbatim:

> *"Cryptocurrency apps may not offer currency for **completing tasks**, such
> as downloading other apps, encouraging other users to download, posting to
> social networks, **etc.**"* — 3.1.5(v)

Two things about that text:

- **It says "completing tasks", not "completing tasks by chance".** Clearing a
  match-3 level is a task. Answering a quiz question is a task. In ordinary
  English these are squarely inside the words.
- **The list ends in "etc."** It is illustrative, not exhaustive. The three
  named examples are promotional, which is why the referral question was the
  sharp one — but nothing in the rule limits it to promotion.

**Skill-versus-chance is a distinction from gambling law, and 3.1.5(v) is not
a gambling rule.** It does not mention chance at all. Where skill does matter
is §5.3, and there it cuts the other way: 5.3.4 says *"Lottery apps must have
consideration, chance, and a prize"*, so removing chance stops it being a
lottery — while doing nothing about 3.1.5(v).

So the skill framing answers a question Apple is not asking here.

### And it would pull in rules that do not currently apply

Today the arcade is outside all of this because XP has no value. Awarding a
tradeable asset for performance would engage, at minimum:

- **5.3.1 / 5.3.2** — if it is a contest, the developer must sponsor it and
  the official rules must appear in the app, stating Apple is not involved.
- **3.1.5(i) and (iii)** — an app that distributes cryptocurrency starts to
  look like a wallet or an exchange, both of which carry organisation
  enrolment and licensing requirements.
- **Prize-competition law**, US state by state, and tax treatment of awards to
  recipients.
- **Whether DGD is security-like**, which changes what distributing it as
  compensation means.

**And note where this leaves B1.** Referrals already pay DGD, so the app is
already exposed under 3.1.5(v). Adding game-based awards would not open a new
front — it would enlarge the one that is currently the critical path, while
that path is still undecided. Doing both at once is the worst ordering.

### "Validation credits" — the name is not the question

Only one thing decides it: **can the credit become anything of value, ever, by
any route?**

| | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Credits are internal recognition, with **no** path to DGD, money, goods, discounts or priority — now or later | **Fine.** This is what XP already is. Rename it if the word helps. |
| Credits convert to DGD at any point — now, later, manually, at a "redemption", as airdrop weighting, as priority to buy | **Same rule, one step added.** Substance governs, not vocabulary. |

The second row is the trap, and it is worth naming plainly: a credit designed
to *feel* like it accrues value while being *called* something else is the
weakest available position. It carries the compliance exposure of a reward
system **and** the user disappointment of turning out to be worthless — and if
it ever does convert, the earlier framing reads as having been deliberate.

The term itself is a small flag. "Validation credits" sounds like it accrues
toward something. If it does not, the arcade's existing vocabulary — XP,
badges, *"Educational only. XP and badges have no monetary value."* — is
already doing the job and is already defensible.

### The coherent options

There are two, and the space between them is where the risk lives.

1. **Genuinely non-monetary recognition.** XP, badges, standing, streaks,
   certification of completion, early access to non-monetary features. Costs
   nothing, already built, already tested, already worded correctly.
2. **Real value for real performance**, designed as such with counsel: rules
   in the app, eligibility and geo-restriction, tax handling, and an answer on
   DGD's legal character. A serious piece of work, not a naming decision.

If the goal behind the question is engagement, option 1 plus better *feedback*
— visible progress, standings, recognition, unlocks — usually does more for
retention than a small payout would, and carries none of this.

If the goal is distribution of DGD, that is a route-to-market question and the
app is the wrong vehicle for it while 3.1.5(v) is unresolved.

## Asked 2026-09-21: could DGD go to top scorers off-app, if the app never mentions it?

Structurally this is the same move as the referral defence — the app offers
nothing, something off-app pays. It is thin there and thinner here.

**Where it is genuinely stronger:** no download-encouragement element. The
referral QR matches 3.1.5(v)'s own example word for word. A top-score award
does not.

**Where it is weaker, and this is the substance:** the rewarded activity
happens *entirely inside the app*. A referral has an off-app leg — the friend
signs up on the website. A top score has none. There is no off-app conduct
being compensated, only in-app play with the ledger entry written elsewhere.
"Completing tasks" describes it precisely, and the only thing between it and
the prohibited thing is bookkeeping location.

### "Not mentioned in the app" makes it worse

The arcade states on screen: *"Educational only. XP and badges have no
monetary value."* If scores convert to DGD, **that sentence is false.**

Silence is one thing; a disclaimer that contradicts the actual programme is
another. It reads as concealment rather than compliance, and it is the kind of
finding that turns a rejection into a scrutinised account. Removing the line
means mentioning the programme — so the "unmentioned" version does not exist
as a stable state.

### And it cannot stay unmentioned

- **Unmentioned means no engagement value.** A secret reward retains nobody,
  which defeats the reason for asking.
- **It would be announced somewhere** — site, socials, Discord, email.
  Reviewers read the website, and ours is linked from the app *and* is the
  app's data source. That is exactly the QR problem: the app pointing at a
  site that does the thing.
- **Winners talk.** The first payout is public.

### One hard engineering fact — R2

From the dynamic audit: **mini-game scores are whatever the client asserts**,
bounded only by the per-round and per-day caps. It is an *accepted* risk
specifically because nothing of value currently rides on a score.

Award DGD on that leaderboard and the payout goes to whoever edits the request
first, not to the best player. Making this buildable at all requires
server-authoritative scoring — deterministic replay or move validation — which
is real work on both the server and the game, and is not on any current plan.

### Sequencing

B1 is unresolved and it is the critical path. This adds a second instance of
the same undecided question before the first is answered. If counsel concludes
the off-app-payout theory does not hold, both fall together — and the second
one arrives having been designed to be invisible.

### What would work

- **In-app: non-monetary recognition only.** Standings, streaks, badges,
  unlocks. Built, tested, correctly worded, zero exposure.
- **Off-app: a contest not keyed to in-app activity.** Website entry, website
  skill task, developer-sponsored with published rules — 5.3.1/5.3.2
  territory, a known and solvable shape. The app never rewards, references or
  depends on it.

**The distinction that matters is not whether the app mentions the award. It is
whether in-app activity is what earns it.**

---

## What I would flag to whoever decides

- **The iOS path is the constrained one.** B2b (the arcade embed) is already
  deferred; this now sits in front of iOS as well.
- **Android is probably not blocked**, but "probably" is doing real work in
  that sentence and it is counsel's to confirm, not mine.
- **A and C are independent.** Doing both costs half a day and settles the
  question completely. Doing C alone costs nothing in engineering and settles
  it almost as well.
- The decision is reversible in one direction only: shipping with invites and
  being rejected is much more expensive than shipping without them and adding
  them back later once the programme's shape is settled.
