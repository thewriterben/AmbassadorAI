# B1 — referrals pay DGD. What now.

**2026-09-21.** DGD has confirmed that referrals are compensated in DGD or in
something convertible to it. This sets out the options and what each costs.

**Not legal advice.** The application of an App Store guideline to an off-app
payout is exactly the kind of question DGD's counsel should answer. What
follows is the engineering picture and an honest read of the risk, so that the
decision is made with the costs visible.

---

## The rule, and why the app is exposed

> *"Cryptocurrency apps may not offer currency for completing tasks, such as
> downloading other apps, encouraging other users to download, posting to
> social networks, etc."* — App Store Review Guidelines, 3.1.5(v)

| Element | Does it apply? |
|---|---|
| "Cryptocurrency apps" | Yes. A DGD ticker with a DGD member path. |
| "may not offer currency" | The **app** offers nothing. The **site** pays. This is the entire defence. |
| "encouraging other users to download" | Yes, and literally. |

The third row is the problem. **Display QR encodes
`https://digitalgold.co/app?ref=USERNAME`** — an app-download link carrying a
referral code that now earns DGD. The guideline's own example is "encouraging
other users to download". This is not analogous to the prohibited thing; it is
the prohibited thing, with the payout moved one hop away.

The "the app doesn't pay" argument is real but thin. The app ships the
mechanism, the code, the QR and the share sheet. Only settlement happens
elsewhere. A reviewer told that the programme pays in DGD is being asked to
accept that the app is a bystander to a system it is the front door for.

**A rejection here is not a normal rejection.** 3.1.5 sits in the section
reviewers treat as a policy matter rather than a bug, it invites scrutiny of
the rest of the submission, and a second attempt arrives with history attached.

### Google Play

Apple's clause is explicit. I could not find an equivalent explicit clause in
Play policy, and Play does host crypto apps with referral programmes — but
store listings are not policy text, and I am not willing to tell you Play is
safe on that basis. Counsel should check Play's Financial Services and
incentivised-installs policies directly before Android is treated as clear.

## Options

Ordered by how much they reduce risk, not by how much anyone will enjoy them.

### A. Remove invites from the app entirely

Invite Friends section, QR, Copy Invite Link, Copy Invite Code, the social
share buttons. Referral continues on the website, where the guideline does not
reach.

- **Engineering:** roughly half a day, both platforms. The section is
  self-contained in `SignupPreviewScreen`; `DigitalGoldSite`'s URL builders
  lose their callers. Tests and reviewer notes to update.
- **Residual risk:** lowest. The reviewer notes become trivially true again —
  the app does not reward, encourage downloads, or carry a referral code.
- **Cost:** a feature someone asked for, and the in-app viral loop.

### B. Remove the app-download QR only

Keep Copy Invite Link (`/signup?ref=`); drop Display QR (`/app?ref=`).

- **Engineering:** about two hours. Delete `InviteQRCode` usage and the
  `appInviteURL` builder.
- **Residual risk:** **partial, and I would not rely on it.** It removes the
  most literal match, but a signup referral that pays DGD still plausibly
  reads as "encouraging other users to download" once the payout is known.
- Reasonable only as a supplement to C, not as a substitute for A.

### C. DGD stops paying DGD for referrals

Pay nothing, or pay in something with no monetary value and no conversion path
— the same standing the arcade's XP already has.

- **Engineering:** none. Not a code change.
- **Residual risk:** lowest of all, and it makes the original answer "no"
  truthfully rather than by argument.
- **Cost:** entirely commercial, and DGD's call. Worth noting that if referral
  compensation is load-bearing for growth, A plus C together still leave the
  website free to run the programme — it is only the app that must be clean.

### D. Submit as-is and argue it

- **Engineering:** none now.
- **Residual risk:** highest, and asymmetric. If it lands, nothing was spent.
  If it does not, the cost is a policy-level rejection on a submission that is
  otherwise in good shape, plus rework under time pressure and a worse
  starting position for the next attempt.

---

## What I would flag to whoever decides

- **The iOS path is the constrained one.** B2b (the arcade embed) is already
  deferred; this now sits in front of iOS as well.
- **Android is probably not blocked**, but "probably" is doing real work in
  that sentence and it is counsel's to confirm, not mine.
- **A and C are independent.** Doing both costs half a day and settles the
  question completely. Doing C alone costs nothing in engineering and settles
  it almost as well.
- The decision is reversible in one direction only: shipping with invites and
  being rejected is much more expensive than shipping without them and adding
  them back later once the programme's shape is settled.
