# DEEP SOUTH FOCUSED SWEEP — AL / MS / LA / AR

**Run date: 2026-08-12.** Narrow sweep: active crypto / blockchain / Web3 / bitcoin student clubs, and how fast an outside cryptocurrency project can buy or borrow a table there in Fall 2026.

**Verification convention.** Every cell carries a source URL. `UNVERIFIED` = not established from a primary source — never a guess. `DIRECTORY UNREADABLE` = the campus org directory is JavaScript-rendered, robots-disallowed or login-gated, so a club may exist and be invisible to this method; that is explicitly **not** a finding of absence. **No officer name, phone number, fee or date below was written from memory.**

**Headline.** Three campuses in these four states have a confirmed crypto/blockchain student club, and all three are in **Louisiana and Arkansas**. Alabama and Mississippi produced **zero confirmed clubs** — but every major directory in both states was unreadable, so that is a measurement failure, not a verdict. The single best find is the **University of Arkansas**, which pairs a demonstrably alive, contactable club with a printed **$100 non-university table / $0 if a student org reserves it** — the exact ten-to-one "get the club to sign" shape flagged in the Kansas precedent.

## 1. TOP PICKS

Ranked on the intersection that matters: **a club that is provably alive × a route in that can be bought or sponsored quickly.**

### 1. University of Arkansas (Fayetteville) — the only campus in four states where both halves are fully proven.

This is the sweep's winner and it is not close. It is the **only** campus of the sixteen where I could read the club's own page rather than a platform shell: **145 members, 6 named officers, a named faculty advisor, a published club email, and 13 newsletters sent.** That is a live organization with a mailbox someone actually checks — `uarkcryptohogs@gmail.com`.

And the access route is printed, in dollars, in a PDF:

| Arkansas Union "Information Table" | Rate |
|---|---|
| Student Affairs / RSOs | **NO CHARGE** |
| University Department | $50 |
| Official University Sponsored Event | $100 |
| **Non-University** | **$100** |

That is **exactly the Kansas shape** — a printed penalty for showing up unsponsored and a printed zero for showing up with a student org. The delta is smaller in absolute dollars than Kansas's $20-vs-$200, but the sponsored side here is *free*, which makes the ratio infinite. **The play is unambiguous: get Crypto Hogs to reserve the table.** Email the club, co-brand the event, let the RSO hold the reservation, and the university's own price list charges you nothing.

The unsponsored fallback is also cheap and fast: **$100 and three business days' notice**, capped at five reservations per semester. Watch the **$1M CGL insurance** trigger — it attaches to indoor facilities of 100+ capacity, so an open-area information table may avoid it, but confirm on the call. Also note the **$10 no-show fee and the three-strikes rule** — do not book speculatively.

Arkansas is additionally the **friendliest of the four states on crypto regulation**: its money-transmission statute expressly covers virtual currency, but the state has issued **no-action letters** to digital-asset businesses including wallet providers and bitcoin sellers — the only such flexibility among AL/MS/LA/AR.

**Call (479) 575-2146** (Union reservationists) and **479-575-5255** (Student Involvement, to confirm the club's Fall 2026 registration status and current officers before you rely on the roster above).
Sources: [club](https://hogsync.uark.edu/uarkcryptoclub/home/) · [FY26 price list PDF](https://union.uark.edu/meetings-and-events/arkansas_union-pricing_fy26.pdf) · [FAMA 7080](https://policies.uark.edu/fayetteville-policies/fama/7080.php) · [Union contacts](https://union.uark.edu/meetings-and-events/reservation-policies-and-banner-space.php)

### 2. Tulane — the best *club* in the region, behind a wall with exactly one door in it.

Tulane Fintech is the most sponsor-receptive organization found in these four states: it is the **founding chapter of the Crypto Current Network**, teaches blockchain modules and coding workshops, carries a nine-person exec board, and as of Feb 2025 was **"planning a sponsored conference for 2026."** A crypto project looking for a partner rather than a table should read that sentence twice — the club is *already shopping for sponsors*.

But read the tabling rule carefully, because it is the most explicitly anti-fronting language in the sweep. Tulane does not merely say outside vendors can't table; it closes the workaround in the same breath: RSOs **"cannot table on behalf of an unaffiliated individual, group, organization, company, or vendor."** That kills the Kansas play outright at the tabling locations.

The door that *is* open is a genuinely good one and it costs **nothing**: off-campus vendors are permitted in the LBC **at non-tabling spaces, at no charge, in partnership with an RSO or department**, with a club rep present the entire time. So the correct posture at Tulane is not "buy a table" — it's **"co-host with Tulane Fintech in a reserved non-tabling space."** Stay inside your marked area, display both names, and do not approach passers-by; the policy bans roaming and door-to-door solicitation.

Classes start **Aug 17, 2026** — the earliest of the three, so this is the first deadline on the calendar. If you fall back to the unsponsored Facility Use route instead, budget **45 days** — meaning a sponsored-in-September event needs the club conversation started now.

**Call 504-865-5190** (LBC Information Desk); email **bgriffith@tulane.edu** to reach the RSO office and **dnoveck@tulane.edu** for reservations.

## 2. BLOCKERS

**A. Tulane's anti-fronting clause — the hardest single rule in the sweep.**
Most campuses that ban outside tabling leave the sponsored workaround implicit. Tulane forecloses it in writing: non-Tulane companies and vendors "are not eligible" for tabling, **and** RSOs "cannot table on behalf of an unaffiliated … company, or vendor." Do not attempt to have Tulane Fintech book a tabling slot for you. The compliant path is the separately-authorized non-tabling-space partnership, which is free but is a different reservation type. [src](https://lbc.tulane.edu/tabling-map-and-guidelines) · [src](https://lbc.tulane.edu/policies)

**B. Insurance thresholds at both flagships.** LSU requires a **$1,000,000** COI naming *the Board of Supervisors of Louisiana State University A&M* as additional insured — the named-insured wording matters and a generic certificate will bounce. Arkansas requires **$1M CGL** for indoor facilities of 100+ capacity. Get the broker moving before the booking call, not after. [LSU](https://www.lsu.edu/auxiliary-services/services/book-spaces/reservation-guidelines-off-campus-entities.php) · [UARK](https://policies.uark.edu/fayetteville-policies/fama/7080.php)

**C. Lead-time spread is 2 to 45 days — plan per campus, not per region.** Tulane RSO tabling **2 business days** · Arkansas non-university **3 business days** · LSU off-campus **15 business days** · Tulane unsponsored Facility Use **45 days** (plus 30 for the rental agreement, 10 for insurance). With Tulane starting Aug 17 and LSU Aug 24, the unsponsored Tulane route is **already effectively closed** for early-semester dates as of this run date.

**D. Arkansas's reservation cap and no-show penalty.** Non-University entities get **5 reservations per semester**, then no more than **3 held at a time**; and a **$10 no-show fee** with **privileges revoked after three**. A multi-stop campus tour cannot treat Fayetteville as an unlimited venue. [src](https://policies.uark.edu/fayetteville-policies/fama/7080.php) · [src](https://involvement.uark.edu/registered_student_organizations/event-planning.php)

**E. Louisiana Virtual Currency Business Act — the one state-level regime that genuinely bites in these four states.**
Louisiana enacted a dedicated VCBA requiring an **NMLS license** for "virtual currency business activity": exchanging, transferring or storing virtual currency; holding electronic precious metals; exchanging digital game value. A lighter **registration** tier exists for entities whose annual Louisiana virtual-currency volume will not exceed **$35,000**. There are extensive exceptions, notably for securities-regulated virtual currency and **personal/academic use**. Critically for a tabling operation: **marketing or promoting a crypto project is not among the enumerated regulated activities** — but *operating a wallet, moving user funds, or executing an exchange at the table* would be. There is also a live interpretive overlap: **OFI maintains older guidance treating virtual-currency exchangers as money transmitters**, so two frameworks may be asserted at once. **If your booth does anything transactional in Louisiana, get counsel before Aug 17.** [OFI guidance PDF](https://ofi.la.gov/ofi-docs/NonDepVirtualCurrencyRegulationGuidance.pdf) · [OFI emergency rule](https://ofi.la.gov/NonDepVirtualCurrencyEmerpencyRule_112022.pdf) · [Bloomberg Law state survey](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) · [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2022/2022-11-21-louisianas-virtual-currency-licensing-law-taking-effect-finally) · [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/blockchain-legal-resource/louisiana-adopts-virtual-currency-business-act)

**F. All four states fold virtual currency into money transmission.** Alabama licenses "selling or issuing payment instruments, stored value, or receiving money or monetary value for transmission," and virtual currencies fall inside "monetary value." Mississippi defines monetary value as "a medium of exchange, whether or not redeemable in money," expressly reaching digital assets. Arkansas's statute expressly includes virtual currency — **but Arkansas has issued no-action letters** to certain digital-asset businesses, making it the most permissive of the four. [src](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/)

**G. Credit-card / financial-product marketing statutes — NOT FOUND in any of the four states, with a caveat.**
A targeted search for a Minnesota §135A.145 / Pennsylvania 24 P.S. §23-2302-A / West Virginia §18B-14-10 / N.J.S.A. 18A:3C-1 analogue in **Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana or Arkansas returned no such statute.** Results surfaced only the Connecticut and New York/CUNY analogues and the federal rule. Alabama's nearest hit, **Ala. Code §8-19A-14**, is a **telemarketing** contract-and-cancellation provision, not a campus-marketing ban. **Record this as NOT FOUND, not as proven absent** — a single search cannot clear four state codes. [CT survey](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2002/rpt/2002-R-0100.htm) · [Ala. §8-19A-14](https://codes.findlaw.com/al/title-8-commercial-law-and-consumer-protection/al-code-sect-8-19a-14/) · [GAO report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-14-225.pdf)

**H. The federal floor still applies everywhere.** **12 CFR §1026.57(c)** bars offering tangible items to induce a college student to apply for open-end credit **on campus or within 1,000 feet of campus**. Swag-for-signup mechanics tied to any credit product are off the table at all sixteen campuses regardless of state law. [src](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/57/)

**I. No campus rule found reaching payment credentials or on-site app sign-ups.** None of the readable policies at Arkansas, LSU or Tulane addressed collecting payment credentials, app installs, or executing agreements at a table. **This is an unresolved question, not a green light** — ask it explicitly on each permission call.

**J. Tulane's mandatory catering monopoly.** Any LBC event with food must use **Olive Blue Catering** exclusively (504-865-5254). Arkansas has the parallel rule: the Union requires **Ozark Catering Company** for all food. Do not plan to bring refreshments. [Tulane](https://lbc.tulane.edu/event-policies) · [UARK](https://involvement.uark.edu/registered_student_organizations/event-planning.php)
