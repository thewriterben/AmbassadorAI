## Cross-Campus Headline Findings

**1. No California campus in scope has started Fall 2026 as of Aug 12, 2026 — but three start within 12 days.** SJSU opens **Aug 19**; Cal Poly SLO, SDSU, USC and CSULB all open **Aug 24**; Berkeley instruction **Aug 26**. The six quarter campuses do not begin instruction until **Sept 22–24**, and UCSB's quarter opens **Sept 20**. This is a two-wave tour, not one sweep, and the gap between wave 1 and wave 2 is **five weeks**.

**2. Cal Poly San Luis Obispo has converted from quarters to SEMESTERS effective Fall 2026.** It was the last CSU campus on the quarter calendar; the Chancellor's Office mandated conversion in October 2021 and it takes effect "by the start of the 2026-2027 academic year." Cal Poly therefore moves from a *late-September* start to an **Aug 24** start — it jumps from near-last to near-first in the tour order. Any tour plan built on Cal Poly being a quarter school is wrong by five weeks. (https://semesters.calpoly.edu/ ; https://registrar.calpoly.edu/fall-semester)

**3. The California policy picture is the most closed of any state researched so far, and it is closed at three layers.** Above campus policy sit (a) UC systemwide PACAOS-30, (b) the CSU Interim Systemwide Time, Place and Manner policy, and (c) the UC Regents' Regulations Governing Conduct of Non-Affiliates. Below them, **every single one of the 12 campuses restricts tabling to campus-affiliated entities.** There is no California campus in this set where an outside commercial entity can simply buy a table on the main quad the way UArizona's ASUA Vendor Fair permits.

**4. Exactly one campus publishes a price list for outside commercial marketing: UC Davis.** UC Davis P&P 270-25 sets sponsorship fees at **$1,000 minimum for on-site marketing** by a commercial entity ($600 for qualifying local businesses), **$500 minimum for logo exposure only**, and $400–$700 for student-organized career fairs. This is the only published, priced, legitimate front-door in the state. (https://supplychain.ucdavis.edu/up3/commercial-activities-policy)

**5. The single best opportunity in California is not a campus at all — it is Cal Hacks 13.0.** Run by Hackathons at Berkeley, **Fall 2026, San Francisco, 3,000+ hackers from 300+ universities**, with a published sponsorship ladder: **Bronze $10K / Silver $20K / Golden Bear $35K / Anchor $50K**. Every tier includes a booth and resume access; higher tiers add API workshops, prize tracks and stage time. Contact **sponsorship@hackberkeley.org**. Because it is a private student-run event held off-campus, it sidesteps UC Berkeley's commercial-activity prohibition entirely. (https://hackberkeley.org/sponsorship.pdf)

**6. Two campuses name payment credentials or credit-card marketing specifically.** UCSB Campus Regulations require credit card vendors to "abide by UCOP and UCSB guidelines regulating the on-campus marketing of credit cards to students." UCSD's Library Walk policy bars information tables from "Solicitation of donations whether monetary or physical property." These are the closest California analogues to the payment-credential language flagged in the brief.

**7. Anti-"fronting" language is explicit at three campuses and will close the club-sponsorship workaround.** SDSU: "No organization may reserve space on behalf of another organization/department, sell, sublease, or transfer services to another individual or organization." SJSU: "No co-sponsorships are allowed." UC Berkeley §224: "The reserving organization may not transfer control of the event... to any other organization, group, or individual speaker."

**8. A correction to the brief's premise on the Leonard Law.** §66301 covers **public** institutions; the private-institution provision is **Education Code §94367**. Either way it protects *students* from institutional discipline and confers nothing on an outside commercial actor.

**9. No audience mismatches in this set.** All 12 campuses have substantial undergraduate business and/or computer science populations. (UCSF, the UC system's health-sciences-only campus, is correctly absent from the list.)

# STATE-LEVEL POLICY LAYER

## UC SYSTEMWIDE — PACAOS-30, Policy on Speech and Advocacy
**Revised July 28, 2004.** https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/2710523/PACAOS-30

⚠️ The canonical UCOP URL returned **"Too many redirects"** on direct fetch. Text retrieved from FIRE's mirror: https://www.fire.org/sites/default/files/2024/11/UC%20PACAOS-30_%20Policy%20on%20Speech%20and%20Advocacy.pdf

- **§30.10** — "The University is committed to assuring that all persons may exercise the constitutionally protected rights of free expression, speech, assembly, and worship."
- **§30.30** — "The time, place, and manner of exercising the constitutionally protected rights of free expression, speech, assembly, and worship are subject to campus regulations that shall provide for non-interference with University functions and reasonable protection to persons against practices that would make them involuntary audiences or place them in reasonable fear, as determined by the University, for their personal safety."
- **§30.30.A** — "Orderly behavior and the normal conduct of University affairs shall govern time, place, and manner of exercising free speech and advocacy."

**Operationally important:** PACAOS-30 contains **no** provisions on commercial solicitation, non-affiliate access, or outside-organization sponsorship. Section IV delegates all of that to **campus implementing regulations**. For this tour, PACAOS-30 is background; the binding text is always the campus regulation.

**Companion systemwide document:** *Regulations Governing Conduct of Non-Affiliates in the Buildings and on the Grounds of the University of California* — https://policy.ucop.edu/doc/3000127/NonAffiliateRegs. Cited as binding by UCLA Policy 850, UCI Campus Access Regulations, and UCSD PPM 510-1 §IX. **Full text UNVERIFIED — not fetched.**

## CALIFORNIA CAMPUS FREE-SPEECH STATUTE — the Leonard Law

⚠️ **The brief's premise needs correcting.** Per FIRE (https://www.fire.org/research-learn/enacted-campus-free-speech-statutes-california):

- **Education Code §66301** applies to "the governing boards of **public** institutions" — the Regents of the University of California, the Trustees of the CSU, and community college district governing boards. Prohibits them from "making or enforcing rules that would authorize discipline for students engaging in speech or conduct that would be protected from governmental restrictions under the First Amendment... or Section 2 of Article I of the California Constitution."
- **Education Code §94367** is the provision covering **private** non-religious institutions — i.e. **Stanford and USC**. "Contains equivalent protections, with an exemption for private institutions that are controlled by a religious organization."
- Both create "a cause of action allowing students to file lawsuits to enforce their rights."
- **AB 2581 (2006)** bars prior restraints on the student press at public and private institutions.
- Text of §66301: https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/education-code/edc-sect-66301/

**Why this matters far less than it appears:** the Leonard Law protects **enrolled students** against **discipline by their own institution**. It confers no right of access on a non-affiliated commercial entity. **Its practical use is indirect:** it strengthens a *student club* that wants to host DGD as a speaker at both private campuses.

**Reading:** Sproul Plaza is the most famous public forum in American higher education, and *tabling on it is closed to outside entities by rule*. §231 appears to open a sponsorship door — but §224 slams it. Sponsorship mitigates but does not cure.

## (4) California's Campus Free-Speech Statute
- **Ed Code §66301** — Leonard Law, **PUBLIC** institutions (UC Regents, CSU Trustees, community college boards).
- **Ed Code §94367** — **PRIVATE** non-religious institutions (Stanford, USC).
- Both create a private cause of action. **AB 2581 (2006)** bars prior restraints on student press.
- ⚠️ **Correction to the brief:** private-institution coverage is §94367, not §66301.
- **Tactical value is indirect:** strengthens a *student club's* position hosting DGD at a private campus. Brief the clubs; do not brief the administration.
- Also referenced in UC guidance: **SB 108** ⚠️ contents not retrieved.
