## STATE-LEVEL FINDINGS — read before any campus section

### 1. There is NO Minnesota campus free-speech statute

Minn. Stat. ch. 135A (Postsecondary Education) was read section-by-section. Its full
headnote list contains **no section on free expression, free speech, expressive activity,
public forum, or student speech**. Minnesota has not enacted a FORUM/Goldwater-style
campus-speech act of the kind Oklahoma (70 O.S. § 2120), Arizona, Colorado and roughly
twenty other states have.

- https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/135A/full

**Consequence for DGD:** at the four public campuses in this set (U of M Twin Cities,
U of M Duluth, and the Minnesota State campuses), the only backstop is direct First
Amendment public-forum doctrine — there is no state statute to cite, no statutory ban on
free-speech zones, and no statutory outdoor-forum guarantee. At the four private campuses
(St. Thomas, Macalester, Carleton, Augsburg) there is no forum obligation at all. **Do not
tell anyone Minnesota has a campus free-speech law. It does not.**

### 2. ⚠⚠ Minn. Stat. § 135A.145 — SALE OF STUDENT INFORMATION; MARKETING CREDIT CARDS

This is the single most important statute in the state for this project, and it is easy to
miss because it sits in the "Miscellaneous" block of ch. 135A.

Verbatim scope: the section applies to a "**public or private postsecondary educational
institution, including its agents, employees, student or alumni organizations, or
affiliates.**"

It prohibits two things:

1. selling, giving, or otherwise transferring **to any card issuer** the name, address,
   telephone number or other contact information **of an undergraduate student** without
   that student's written permission; and
2. entering into **"any agreement to market credit cards to undergraduate students."**

"Credit," "credit card" and "card issuer" are defined by reference to the federal Truth in
Lending Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1602. Enforcement is by the Minnesota Attorney General under
Minn. Stat. § 8.31.

- https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/135A.145

**Why this matters:** it reaches **student organizations and affiliates**, not just the
institution. It is almost certainly the origin of the two campus rules below, which are
otherwise unusual:

- St. Cloud State bans, in the promotional spaces, "**credit card or debit card
  sign-ups**" (https://www.stcloudstate.edu/atwood/about/policies/advertising-promotion.aspx)
- Augsburg's vendor/tabling policy bans "**credit card promotions**"
  (https://sites.augsburg.edu/events/policies/vendor-tabling-policy/)

DGD is not a card issuer under TILA and a wallet is not a credit card, but the statutory
language is broad enough that any Minnesota campus lawyer will reach for it the moment a
financial-product table appears. **An ambassador should be able to say, unprompted: "We are
not a card issuer under 15 U.S.C. 1602 and we are not signing anyone up for credit."**
Do not collect card numbers, do not run sign-ups that look like card sign-ups.

### 3. Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCDPA), Minn. Stat. §§ 325M.10–.21

Effective **July 31, 2025**. Applicability, per the Minnesota Attorney General's own
announcement: a business is covered if it "**control[s] or process[es] the personal data of
100,000 or more Minnesota residents, or if they earn over 25% of their revenue from the
sale of personal data and process or control personal data of 25,000 consumers or more.**"
Certain education-technology providers are separately covered.

- https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2025/07/28_MCDPA.asp
- https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/325M.10 (citation section only)
- https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/325M.11 (definitions only — the numeric
  thresholds are not in .10 or .11; the AG page is the retrievable source)

**Realistic read for DGD:** the 100,000-Minnesotan prong will not be met by a campus tour.
The 25%-of-revenue + 25,000-consumer prong is the one to watch if DGD monetises data. The
practical exposure is not the threshold — it is that MCDPA gives Minnesotans a right to
know, delete and opt out, and gives the AG an enforcement hook. **Collect the minimum at a
table: an email address and nothing else. Do not collect phone numbers, wallet addresses
and IDs at the same time.**

### 4. Minnesota crypto legislation — a live, favourable backdrop

- **HF 3709 (2026)** — signed by Gov. Tim Walz, **effective August 1, 2026**. Authorises
  Minnesota **state-chartered banks and credit unions to hold virtual currency and the
  cryptographic keys** that control it on behalf of customers and members. Requires written
  risk-management/cybersecurity policies, 60-days' advance written notice to the Minnesota
  Commissioner of Commerce, and strict segregation of client digital assets. Minnesota
  joins New York, Wyoming and Virginia. This is **eleven days old** as of the tour window
  and is an excellent conversation opener.
  https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/minnesota-law-opens-crypto-custody
- **Minnesota Bitcoin Act**, SF 2661 / HF 2946 (94th Legislature, 2025–26), authored by
  Sen. Jeremy Miller — would allow payment to the state in cryptocurrency and authorise the
  State Board of Investment to invest in cryptocurrency. **Introduced, not enacted.**
  https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/text.php?number=SF2661&version=0&session=ls94&session_year=2025&session_number=0
  http://www.house.leg.state.mn.us/bills/Info/HF2946

### 5. TWO SEPARATE SYSTEMS — do not conflate them

Minnesota's public higher education is split between two entirely separate governing
bodies with different boards and different policy books.

**(a) University of Minnesota system — Board of Regents.**
Campuses in this set: **Twin Cities, Duluth.** Policy layer is the University Policy
Library (policy.umn.edu) plus, for tabling, the Student Unions & Activities Student Group
Policies. Governing documents actually retrieved:
- *Using and Leasing University Outdoor Space: Twin Cities* — effective **August 2008**,
  responsible officers VP for University Services and VP for Student Affairs.
  https://policy.umn.edu/operations/outdoor and appendix https://policy.umn.edu/operations/outdoor-appa
- *Major Events* — effective **September 2020**. https://policy.umn.edu/operations/majorevents
- *Academic Calendars: Crookston, Morris, Rochester, Twin Cities* — sets the 70–75
  instruction-day rule. https://policy.umn.edu/education/academiccalendar
- SUA Student Group Policies (the operative anti-fronting text). https://sua.umn.edu/student-group-policies
- **There is no free-standing "solicitation" policy in the UMN Policy Library.** Searches
  for one returned nothing; the operative language lives in the SUA Student Group Policies
  and the outdoor-space policy. That is itself a finding — an ambassador told "it's against
  university policy" should ask *which* policy, because the answer is a student-union
  bylaw, not a Regents policy.

**(b) Minnesota State — Board of Trustees (33 colleges and universities, 54 campuses).**
Campuses in this set: **Mankato, St. Cloud State, Winona State, Metropolitan State.**
Governing documents actually retrieved:
- **System Procedure 6.7.2, Use of College and University Facilities (College or University
  as Lessor)** — effective **July 28, 1996**, **last revised January 22, 2026**. This is the
  document that governs every outside-entity space use at all four Minnesota State campuses
  below. Operative requirements: "**Users of facilities must have a fully executed, written
  agreement before occupying or using a college or university facility.**" "**Users of a
  college or university facility shall provide evidence of adequate liability insurance
  coverage … naming the State of Minnesota … as an additional insured, prior to using or
  occupying.**" Institutions must "**charge a User a reasonable amount when leasing their
  facilities that covers all costs typically included in a standard market lease…**" All
  agreements must include "**a cancellation clause, which can be invoked at the college's or
  university's sole and absolute discretion.**" Approval thresholds: Vice Chancellor
  approval at $100,000+ or 5+ years; $150,000+ for leasehold improvements.
  https://www.minnstate.edu/board/procedure/607p2.html
- **Board Policy 3.1, Student Rights and Responsibilities** — implementation date
  January 18, 1995, last reviewed March 18, 2026. "Individual students and student
  organizations shall be free to examine and to discuss all questions of interest to them
  and to express opinions publicly and privately." "Students shall have the right to
  assemble, to select speakers, and to discuss issues of their choice." **Note the subject
  of every sentence is *students*. Policy 3.1 confers nothing on an outside for-profit
  entity.** https://www.minnstate.edu/board/policy/301.html
- No system-wide Minnesota State solicitation policy exists; solicitation is delegated to
  each campus. https://www.minnstate.edu/board/policy/index.html

### 6. ⚠ THE TWIN CITIES DENSITY FACT — the single most important planning fact in Minnesota

**Five of the ten campuses in this set sit inside the Minneapolis–St. Paul metro, all within
roughly twenty minutes' drive of each other:**

| Campus | Address | Type |
|---|---|---|
| U of M Twin Cities | 300 Washington Ave SE, Minneapolis | Public |
| Augsburg University | 2211 Riverside Ave, Minneapolis | Private (ELCA) |
| University of St. Thomas | 2115 Summit Ave, St. Paul | Private (Catholic) |
| Macalester College | 1600 Grand Ave, St. Paul | Private |
| Metropolitan State University | 700 E 7th St, St. Paul | Public (Minnesota State) |

U of M Twin Cities to Augsburg is about 1.5 miles. Augsburg to St. Thomas is about 5 miles.
St. Thomas to Macalester is about 1 mile — they are on the same street, Summit/Grand, and
students cross-register between them through the ACTC consortium. Metro State is about 4
miles east of Macalester.

**Operational consequence:** an ambassador based in the Twin Cities for a single week can
work five campuses without a hotel change, and can run *paid, compliant* tables at three of
them (U of M Explore U, Augsburg $75/day, and — one hour away, not in the metro — St. Cloud
$85/day). Duluth is 2.5 hours north, Mankato 1.5 hours southwest, Winona 2 hours southeast,
Northfield (Carleton) 45 minutes south. **Build the tour as a Twin Cities base with three
spokes, not as a linear drive.**

### 7. ⚠ CARLETON IS ON TRIMESTERS AND DIVERGES FROM EVERYTHING ELSE

Carleton's academic year is "**comprised of three 10-week-long terms**"; students "normally
carry three courses, or 18 credits, per term."
https://www.carleton.edu/registrar/academic-term/

Carleton Fall Term 2026 runs **Mon Sep 14 – Mon Nov 23, 2026**. It starts *six days after*
the U of M Twin Cities and *three weeks after* Mankato, St. Cloud and Winona — and it
**ENDS on November 23, before Thanksgiving**. There is no Thanksgiving break because the
term is already over, and there is **no December window at all** at Carleton.

Anyone planning "hit Northfield in early December" will find an empty campus. Carleton is a
**late-September-to-early-November-only** stop.

### 8. Start-date spread — Minnesota has the widest spread of any state in this project

Seventeen days separate the earliest start (Metro State, Sat Aug 22) from the latest
(Carleton, Mon Sep 14). Nine of ten are semester schools; Carleton is the sole trimester.
See the cross-campus table at the end.

## C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY — quoted verbatim

The operative document is **SUA Student Group Policies**, https://sua.umn.edu/student-group-policies

**Article XI, Section 6, Subd 17 — the flat prohibition:**
> "**University policy prohibits the use of University property by non-University entities
> for the purpose of revenue generation or the sale, solicitation, or promotion of goods or
> services.**"

**Article XI, Section 6, Subd 10 — the narrow exception:**
> "**Off-campus agencies, nonprofit organizations, musicians, guest speakers, or performing
> artists shall not be permitted to solicit funds on campus unless they have a contract with
> the University of Minnesota or are sponsored by a student group.**"

**Article XVI, Section 7, Subd 2 — the contact-table bar:**
> "**Student groups are not permitted to reserve contact tables on behalf of University
> departments or external organizations.**"
> (Same subdivision: "Food and beverages or the sales of food and beverages are not
> permitted at any indoor contact tables.")

**Article III, Section 4 — ⚠ ANTI-FRONTING, by name.** Student groups are prohibited from
providing "inappropriate access to student group resources" to non-registered groups,
**a practice the policy itself calls "fronting."** Violations can result in disciplinary
action and a change to the group's registered status.

**SUA Fundraising page — the sharpest sentence for a table challenge:**
> "**Personnel from non-University vendors or companies are not allowed at the contact table,
> when sales or sales-related, fundraising or commercial activities are being conducted.**"
Same page: student groups may conduct on-campus sales/fundraising "**up to 5 days per
semester**"; "**Sales and/or fundraising activities shall not be conducted in classrooms,
campus offices, residential facilities, and/or other University buildings, without written
consent.**" https://sua.umn.edu/fundraising

**Does sponsorship cure it? PARTIALLY, AND NOT FOR A TABLE.** Subd 10 says an off-campus
agency may solicit if "sponsored by a student group." But Article XVI §7 Subd 2 forbids the
student group from reserving the contact table for the external organization, and the
fundraising page forbids the vendor's personnel from standing at it. The reconciliation the
policy contemplates is a *student-group event with a sponsor*, subject to: "**The primary
purpose of the event cannot be non-University sponsor presence, promotion, or sales**";
"**Insurance requirements and sponsorship agreements may be required**"; and "**If
promotional materials, including apparel, are created with sponsor logos, the sponsorship
must be acknowledged via text as to not imply a University partnership.**"

**Space-reservation terms (SUA Event Services).** Three client tiers: Registered Student
Group / University Department / **University Guest**. Coffman Great Hall 0–8 hrs is
$550 / $1,100 / **$2,200** respectively. For non-university clients: "**A non-refundable
deposit of 50% of the room rate is due at the time of booking. The remaining balance is due
at least 30 days prior to the event date.**" Non-University groups must sign a **Facility
Use Agreement** and provide "**proof of insurance**" before reserving — **no dollar limit is
published**. Cancellation ladder: 25% of fee at 91–180 days out, rising to **100% of fee at
0–5 days**.
- https://reservesua.umn.edu/rates-payments · https://reservesua.umn.edu/reservation-policies
- Contact-table rule, verbatim: "The reservation includes a maximum of one six-foot table
  and two chairs, no additional furniture is allowed." "A group is only permitted one contact
  table reservation, for a maximum of five hours, each day."

**Outdoor space.** *Using and Leasing University Outdoor Space: Twin Cities* (Aug 2008).
Assemblies of fewer than 100 participants need no permit at five designated plazas. But
"any gathering by non-University persons or entities (regardless of number of participants)"

pay a flat fee as a part of the Facility Use Agreement they complete with the Real Estate
Office**" — **the flat fee amount is not published**. Student groups and departments pay
nothing. Permit lead times: 14 days standard, 30 days if security/traffic management, 12
weeks for large events/concerts.
- https://policy.umn.edu/operations/outdoor · https://policy.umn.edu/operations/outdoor-appa
- https://outdoor.umn.edu/faq

**Major Events policy** (Sep 2020): "This policy applies to anyone, be it an individual, a
group, an academic department, college, or administrative unit, proposing to host a major
event." Major Event Proposal form; Major Events Committee review "up to 14 business days";
requester must "Pay the University for all allocable costs."
https://policy.umn.edu/operations/majorevents

**Payment credentials / contracts on site:** nothing in the UMN documents reaches payment
apps or on-site contract signing directly. The reachable constraint is the *state* statute,
§ 135A.145 above, which forbids the University **and its student organizations** from any
agreement to market credit cards to undergraduates.

**Public-forum posture:** UMN is a public university, so First Amendment forum doctrine
applies to its traditional public fora. There is **no Minnesota campus free-speech statute**
to cite. The policy's own framing is that the University "recognizes the importance of
fostering diversity, serving the greater community, and acting as a marketplace for ideas" —
aspirational language, not a grant of commercial access.

> Sayles-Hill Great Space Monday-Friday from 9:00 a.m. – 6:00 p.m.**"
> "**We do not accept reservations for local business or off-campus organizations.**"
> ⚠ **BUT, verbatim, immediately after:** "**However, if you receive sponsorship from a campus
> department or organization, the department/organization can make a table reservation on
> your behalf. THERE IS NO FEE IF YOU ARE SPONSORED.**"

**This is the opposite of the anti-fronting rules found at UMN Twin Cities, St. Cloud State
and — in effect — St. Thomas.** Carleton *invites* the arrangement those campuses forbid by
name and criminalise as "fronting." A campus department or a chartered student organization
may reserve the table **on behalf of** an outside entity, and it costs nothing.

Operative mechanics:
- "**Tables need to be reserved 24 hours in advance.**"
- Reservation is via the **EMS Web App using a Carleton ID and password** — so the sponsoring
  department or club must physically make the booking. DGD cannot self-serve.
- "**No tape should be placed on the tables**"; signage must be "placed behind your table
  using the tack strips."
- Sayles-Hill posting policy is separate:
  https://www.carleton.edu/student-activities/guide/event-promotion/posting-policy/

**Carleton is private.** No public-forum obligation, no statute. But the written rule is
unusually generous, and unlike a discretionary "come talk to us," it is a published,
quotable sentence an ambassador can read aloud.

**Not found at Carleton:** any rate card (there is no fee if sponsored), any insurance
requirement, any deposit or cancellation terms, any explicit commercial-solicitation ban, and
any payment-credential or contract-signing language. The CSA Handbook for Student
Organizations (https://www.carleton.edu/orgs/csa/administration/handbook/) contains none of
these either; it only bars non-enrolled and on-leave students from participating in CSA
chartered organizations.

**Sponsorship is not merely permitted — it is the ONLY route, and it is the whole strategy.**

## (4) The state campus free-speech statute

**⚠ THERE IS NONE. Minnesota has not enacted a campus free-speech statute.**

Minn. Stat. ch. 135A was read section by section; no section addresses free expression, free
speech, expressive activity, public forums or student speech.
https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/135A/full

What Minnesota *does* have, and what an ambassador should know instead:

1. ⚠⚠ **Minn. Stat. § 135A.145 — SALE OF STUDENT INFORMATION; MARKETING CREDIT CARDS.**
   Applies to any "**public or private postsecondary educational institution, including its
   agents, employees, student or alumni organizations, or affiliates.**" Prohibits (a)
   transferring an undergraduate's contact information to any card issuer without written
   permission and (b) entering "**any agreement to market credit cards to undergraduate
   students.**" TILA definitions; AG enforcement under § 8.31. **This is why St. Cloud State
   bans "credit card or debit card sign-ups" and Augsburg bans "credit card promotions."**
   *Prepare the answer: DGD is not a card issuer under 15 U.S.C. § 1602 and runs no credit
   sign-ups.*
   https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/135A.145
2. **Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act, Minn. Stat. §§ 325M.10–.21, effective July 31,
   2025.** Covers a business that "control[s] or process[es] the personal data of 100,000 or
   more Minnesota residents, or … earn[s] over 25% of [its] revenue from the sale of personal
   data and process[es] or control[s] personal data of 25,000 consumers or more."
   https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2025/07/28_MCDPA.asp
3. **Minnesota State Board Policy 3.1** protects *student* expression and assembly at the four
   Minnesota State campuses — it confers nothing on an outside entity.
   https://www.minnstate.edu/board/policy/301.html
4. **Minnesota State System Procedure 6.7.2** is the real gate at Mankato, St. Cloud, Winona
   and Metro State: written Facilities Agreement, liability insurance naming the State of
   Minnesota as additional insured, market-rate cost recovery, and cancellation at the
   institution's "sole and absolute discretion." Last revised **January 22, 2026**.
   https://www.minnstate.edu/board/procedure/607p2.html
5. **HF 3709 (2026), effective Aug 1, 2026** — Minnesota state-chartered banks and credit
   unions may custody virtual currency and cryptographic keys. A genuinely useful,
   eleven-day-old opener. https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/minnesota-law-opens-crypto-custody

**Practical consequence:** at the four public campuses, First Amendment forum doctrine applies
directly to traditional public fora, but there is no statute banning free-speech zones and no
statutory outdoor-forum guarantee to wave. At the four private campuses there is no forum
right at all. **Argue fit and pay the fee; do not argue rights.**
