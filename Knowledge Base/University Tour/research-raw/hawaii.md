## STATE-LEVEL FINDINGS (report once, applies across UH campuses)

### UH System governance stack
The University of Hawaiʻi is one system. Authority runs: Hawaiʻi Administrative Rules → Board of Regents Policy → Executive Policy → Administrative Procedure → campus policy. For facilities and solicitation the relevant layers are:

1. **HAR Title 20, Subtitle 1, Chapter 13 — "Use of University-Owned Facilities," §§ 20-13-1 to 20-13-9.** This is the Board of Regents administrative rule and sits above every campus policy. Confirmed to exist via https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/title-20/subtitle-1 . I was not able to read the section text.

2. **Systemwide Time, Place and Manner (TPM) policy — UPDATED, effective 1 July 2026, on an INTERIM basis.** This is the single most important thing on this page for an ambassador. UH replaced a TPM policy that had not been substantially updated in over ten years. Per UH President Wendy Hensel, the revision targets "inconsistent interpretation and enforcement" and makes standards "more precise, transparent and workable."
   - Protected: "Peaceful protests, demonstrations, rallies, speeches, petitions and other forms of constitutionally guaranteed expression remain fully protected."
   - The policy "explicitly preserves the right" of **students, faculty and staff** to spontaneously assemble in generally accessible outdoor areas **without prior approval**. Note the enumerated classes — affiliates. The reporting does not extend that right to non-affiliated entities.
   - Standards cover noise near classrooms/offices, **designated posting areas**, and unobstructed building access.
   - **UH plans community discussions throughout fall 2026 before final adoption.** So the rules a DGD ambassador operates under in Sept–Dec 2026 are *interim and actively being rewritten*. Anything an office tells you by phone in September may not hold in November. Get it in writing.
   - Landing page: https://www.hawaii.edu/tpm-policy/ — **ROBOTS-BLOCKED to me**, must be read manually.
   - The circulated draft was titled *"DRAFT Administrative Procedure, AP 10.xxx Implementation of Time, Place, and Manner Restrictions"* dated 11.14.25, at https://www.hawaii.edu/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/DRAFT_AP_10.XXX_Time_Place_Manner_Restrictions_11.14.25.pdf — **ROBOTS-BLOCKED**. The final AP number is UNVERIFIED; the "10.xxx" placeholder was never resolved on any page I could read.
   - Sources readable: https://bigislandnow.com/2026/06/30/university-of-hawaiʻi-systemwide-updated-time-place-and-manner-policy-and-free-expression/ and https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/06/28/editorial/island-voices/column-policy-protects-free-expression-at-uh/

### Hawaii campus free-speech statute
**I found no Hawaiʻi state campus free-speech statute** (no FORUM-Act-style law, no "free expression on campus" chapter in HRS). Hawaiʻi appears to regulate this entirely through BOR administrative rule and University policy, which is why the June 2026 TPM update was framed as a *policy* fix rather than statutory compliance. Treat this as **UNVERIFIED-NEGATIVE** — my search budget ran out before I could run a targeted HRS query. Check at https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/ before relying on it. The practical consequence either way: at UH the operative constraint is public-forum doctrine plus the interim TPM policy, not a statute.

### Hawaii's cryptocurrency regulatory posture — this is unusual and materially relevant
Hawaii is one of the few states that went from *hostile* to *deregulated* on digital currency, and it is currently in a legislative vacuum.

- **2020–30 June 2024: Digital Currency Innovation Lab (DCIL).** A DCCA/DFI + Hawaii Technology Development Corporation sandbox that let digital currency companies operate without a Hawaii money transmitter license. It was allowed to sunset. https://cca.hawaii.gov/dfi/news-releases/release-hawaii-digital-currency-innovation-lab-to-conclude/

- **Current DFI position (still the live FAQ, dated 9 Feb 2024):** DFI states that after the DCIL concluded, *"digital currency companies will no longer require a Hawaiʻi-issued money transmitter license to conduct business."* The FAQ lists activities that may be conducted **without** a money transmitter license:
  - "Trading of digital currency or assets"
  - "Providing hosted digital currency wallets or digital currency custodial services"
  - "Issuing or redeeming stable coins"
  - "Transferring digital assets from one person to another"
  
  The carve-back: *"Companies that conduct activity in US$ or other fiat currencies that meets the definition of money transmission…will likely require a money transmitter license for that fiat-denominated activity if an exclusion…does not apply."* DFI also notes the list "may change from time to time."
  https://cca.hawaii.gov/dfi/dcil-faq-industry/ and https://cca.hawaii.gov/dfi/dcil-faq-consumers/

- **The 2026 attempt to re-regulate FAILED.** SB 2757 SD1 (33rd Legislature, 2026), *"Relating to Digital Asset Charters,"* would have created a Digital Asset Charter Program under DFI: mandatory charter for digital asset business activity, $9,000 application fee, $1,000 annual renewal, $2,500–$12,500 quarterly assessments, $500,000 minimum tangible net worth, $500,000 surety bond, AML and cybersecurity programs, civil penalties to $20,000 per violation, effective 1 Jan 2027. **It passed the Senate 10 March 2026, was deferred by the House Consumer Protection & Commerce Committee on 17 March 2026, and died in committee.**
  Bill text: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/bills/SB2757_SD1_.PDF — Status: https://legiscan.com/HI/bill/SB2757/2026

**What this means operationally for a campus tour:**
- Hawaii is currently *permissive* on pure-crypto activity — sharply more permissive than most mainland states. No state license is required to trade, custody, or transfer digital assets here.
- But DFI's consumer-facing guidance is a warning, not an endorsement: transactions involving digital currency "are not guaranteed by any government agency" and there is "no government agency that will protect consumer funds." An ambassador who implies Hawaii state oversight or approval is misrepresenting the position.
- The moment DGD touches **fiat** — taking US dollars, on-ramping, anything resembling money transmission in USD — a Hawaii money transmitter license likely attaches. This is a hard line to brief ambassadors on before any campus event where students might hand over cash or use a payment app.
- A charter bill will very likely return in the 2027 session (SB2757 got through the whole Senate). The deregulated window is not guaranteed to persist.

### Inter-island geography — say this plainly
**Mānoa and Hilo are not a drive apart; they are a flight apart.** UH Mānoa is in Honolulu on Oʻahu. UH Hilo is in Hilo on Hawaiʻi Island, ~200 miles southeast across open ocean. Getting between them requires an interisland flight (HNL→ITO). There is no ferry, no bridge, no road. Budget a half-day and an airfare per direction, plus a rental car in Hilo. Hawaiʻi Pacific and Chaminade are both on Oʻahu and are roughly 15–20 minutes apart by car (downtown Honolulu vs. Kaimukī), so those two plus Mānoa are a single trip.

### Scale reality check
Do not plan a four-stop Hawaii tour as though it were four mainland stops. UH Mānoa is the only campus here with a large undergraduate business and CS population. HPU is mid-sized. UH Hilo is small (roughly 3,000 students) and on another island. Chaminade is small (roughly 1,000 undergraduates) and, as documented below, has **zero** business, finance, computer science, or entrepreneurship student organizations. Realistically this is one strong stop, one decent stop, and two that need to justify themselves.

**(iii) Archived predecessor — A1.200, "University of Hawaii at Manoa Facilities Use Policy," effective June 2002.** Readable in full at https://www.hawaii.edu/policy/archives/apm/a1200p/a1200.pdf . **This is ARCHIVED — cite it as background, not as the governing rule.** Verbatim, the operative restrictions:

> "No solicitation shall be conducted in any building, structure, facility, or on any grounds, sidewalks, or streets…"

with enumerated exceptions for vending machines, the bookstore, and **fund-raising by registered student organizations with prior written approval**.

> "The University of Hawai'i at Manoa, as a public institution, is not in competition with other institutions or commercial enterprises…"

> "University-affiliated organizations may sponsor other organizations or individuals and thereby qualify for use of campus facilities…"

> "All non-affiliated organizations are required to provide evidence of adequate insurance protection"

(waivable by the President).

> "The Campus Center Courtyard area is designated as the public forum area for the University of Hawai'i at Manoa where individuals may assemble and engage in public speech activities."

**Reading this for DGD's purposes:**
- **The default is a blanket solicitation ban** on all buildings, grounds, sidewalks and streets. That is broader than most mainland policies, which usually ban only *commercial* solicitation.
- **Sponsorship appears to CURE the access problem rather than being irrelevant.** A1.200 expressly says an affiliated organization may sponsor an outside entity "and thereby qualify for use of campus facilities." There is **no anti-fronting prohibition in A1.200** — the drafters accepted fronting and handled it through liability transfer instead: the sponsoring group assumes responsibility for all damages and must ensure the sponsored entity complies with University rules. Practically, that means the RIO officer who signs for DGD is personally exposing their organization. Ambassadors should say so out loud; it is the honest thing to do and it protects the relationship.
- Sponsorship gets you **facility use**. It does not obviously get you past the **solicitation** ban, which is a separate prohibition with its own narrow exception (RSO fund-raising with prior written approval). Distributing DGD material may be fine; anything transactional probably is not.
- **Insurance:** required of all non-affiliated organizations. **Dollar limits are not stated in A1.200** — they will be in M10.300 or in the Campus Center rental agreement. UNVERIFIED.
- **Fees, deposits, cancellation terms: UNVERIFIED.** The Campus Center rate sheet at https://manoa.hawaii.edu/studentlife/campus-center-complex/meetings-events/planning-an-event/ was ROBOTS-BLOCKED on every attempt.
- **Nothing found reaching payment credentials (credit card, Venmo, payment apps) or signing contracts on site.** A1.200 predates all of that by two decades. Whether M10.300 or the 2026 TPM policy adds such language is UNVERIFIED and is worth asking about directly, since it is exactly the kind of clause a 2025–26 revision would add.
- **Public forum:** Campus Center Courtyard is the designated public-forum area. As a public university, UH is bound by public-forum doctrine there. That is DGD's strongest legal footing at any campus in this state — but note that expressive activity in a public forum is not the same as commercial solicitation, and the solicitation ban is content-neutral on its face.

**Reading this for DGD's purposes — this is the most restrictive regime of the four, and the most explicit:**

- **This is functionally an anti-fronting rule**, though not phrased that way: a club cannot simply reserve space and hand it to DGD, because the *partnership with an unregistered entity* is separately gated on Director approval.

## 2. Outside-entity access, ranked most → least open

**2. UH Mānoa — most open of the actual campuses, with real caveats.**

**3. UH Hilo — same public-forum footing, worse published language, worst logistics.**
Also public, also bound by the systemwide policy. *Against:* its published free-expression page grants permission only for **"non-commercial"** speech, assembly and literature — every permission is qualified that way, which is a clean written basis for refusal; no designated public-forum area identified; the facilities-use procedures are unread; **~3,000 students**; and it requires an interisland flight. Ranked third on principle (public institution) but in practice this is a low-yield stop.

**4. Hawaiʻi Pacific — pure discretion, nothing written either way.**
Private, so no constitutional claim. *For:* I found **no** solicitation or vendor prohibition anywhere on hpu.edu or in the student handbook, so there is nothing written to overcome; a genuine business student population; downtown location; a Student Activity Fee fund that clubs can spend on speakers; and one solid direct line to the deciding office. *Against:* there is equally nothing written to invoke, no appeal, no published fee schedule, no confirmed relevant club, and no club fair on the calendar.

**5. Chaminade — most restrictive, and the audience is not there.**
Private and Catholic. Written rules require: registered-club/department hosting for any unaffiliated speaker; **Director's written approval** to partner with an unregistered entity; **Director + VP of Advancement clearance before any corporate sponsorship is even solicited**; a "values counter to those of the University" discretionary veto; 6 weeks' lead for vendor-contact fundraising; 10 business days for a room, $200 late fee below that. On top of which: **zero business, finance, CS or entrepreneurship student organizations** out of ten total. The one genuine opening is the **Hogan Program's bi-weekly lecture series** — pitch a talk, not a sponsorship, and the approval chain is much shorter.
