## 1. TOP PICKS

Ranked on the intersection that actually matters: **an active club × a route in that can be bought or sponsored quickly.**

**1. Georgia Tech (GA) — the only campus where both halves are fully proven.**
The club is demonstrably alive (current site, live course offering, active socials, a hosted events page), and Georgia Tech is the one campus in all seven states that prints a **retail price list for outside commercial tabling and does not require a student-org sponsor**: $150 indoor half-day is the cheapest confirmed door in the sweep, and $500 buys a full outdoor day. Five business days' lead. The only friction is paperwork — COI, business license, signed Facility Use Agreement 10 days out — so start that packet before booking, not after. Avoid the move-in→Labor Day window unless you want to pay peak ($1,500/day advanced). Phone **404.894.2828** — note this number appears **only in the reservation PDF**, not in any HTML page.
Sources: [tabling + rates](https://studentcenter.gatech.edu/campus-activations-vendor-tabling) · [reservation PDF w/ phone](https://www.studentcenter.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/2025-03/How%20to%20Reserve%20Table%20Spaces%20in%20the%20Student%20Center%2C%20Exhibition%20Hall%2C%20and%20Experiential%20Pathway%20%E2%80%93%20RSO%20%26%20GT%20Departments%203.2025.pdf) · [club](https://www.blockchain-gt.io/)

**2. South Carolina (USC) — best price-per-difficulty, but the club is the open question.**
**$55.00 per use** is the lowest printed table price found anywhere in these seven states, and the policy explicitly contemplates exactly this transaction: a campus entity sponsoring an outside vendor for onsite sale or future-sales activity. The catch is symmetrical to Georgia Tech's: access is cheap and easy, but **no crypto club is confirmed** because Garnet Gate is unreadable. Worth one phone call to Russell House (**803-777-8182**) and one to the Leadership & Service Center to ask whether a crypto/blockchain org is registered — if the answer is yes, this becomes the #1 pick outright.
Sources: [FY26 fees](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/russell_house/documents/FY26_user_fees_final.pdf) · [STAF 3.17 handbook](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/leadership_and_service_center/documents/student_org_handbook/important_policies.pdf)

**3. UNC–Chapel Hill (NC) — a $150 flat-rate door that bypasses the student-org route entirely.**
The Career Center sells for-profit tabling in the Student Union at **$150**, booked through Handshake, no CIO sponsor needed. That is a purchasable, published, non-negotiated route into a flagship. Two constraints: **21 days' lead** (so a fall table needs booking now) and **max two staff**. The Cryptocurrency Club exists in Heel Life but its activity is unverified — and note the framing risk: entering as an *employer* is a recruiting posture, not a marketing one.
Sources: [tabling fee](https://careers.unc.edu/employers/recruitment-opportunities/tabling/) · [club](https://heellife.unc.edu/organization/ccclub)

**4. Duke (NC) — best club in the region, worst front-door.**
Duke Blockchain Club is the single most sponsor-receptive organization found: it is running a two-day conference on **April 3–4, 2026** and openly solicits sponsors and collaborators. But Bryan Center Plaza is closed to external groups outright. **The route here is the club, not the university** — sponsor the conference or a club event and let the students hold the space. Contact contact@dukeblockchain.com.
Sources: [conference](https://www.dukeblockchain.com/) · [plaza restriction](https://eventservices.duke.edu/bryan-center-plaza/)

**5. Virginia Tech (VA) — a $50 floor, but a purpose test you must pass.**
$50 per outdoor location per day is real and cheap, and a Blockchain at Virginia Tech org exists. But VT is the strictest of the "yes" campuses on *why* you're there: an event whose primary purpose is commercial solicitation or advertising alone is not an approved purpose, and Policy 5215 flatly prohibits "uninvited commercial activities." Go in as a co-sponsor of a club event — with the club's rep onsite for the duration, which VT requires anyway. Classes start **Aug 24, 2026**; tabling form is 5 business days out but the policy wants 10 calendar days.
Sources: [non-university fees + sponsorship](https://campuslife.vt.edu/secl_services/event_planning/resources.html) · [Policy 5215](https://policies.vt.edu/5215.pdf)

Two live phone lines: **919-515-3138** and **919-513-8368**.
Sources: [guidelines](https://studentcenters.ncsu.edu/events/resources/guidelines/) · [fair](https://sle.dasa.ncsu.edu/engagement-organizations/involvement-fairs/) · [calendar](https://studentservices.ncsu.edu/calendars/academic/)

## 2. BLOCKERS

### 2a. Campus credit-card / financial-product marketing statutes

- **WEST VIRGINIA — REAL STATUTE, STILL IN FORCE. `W. Va. Code §18B-14-10`, "Credit card solicitation on college campuses; regulation of credit card marketing."** Every governing board must have rules that: **register on-campus credit card marketers**; **limit marketers to specific sites designated by the campus president**; **prohibit marketers from offering tangible gifts in exchange for completing an application**; and bar making any credit application available unless it is **accompanied by a credit card debt education brochure**. Boards must also decide policy on the use of student lists for solicitation. Confirmed live at the legislature's own code site — no repeal notation, unlike the adjacent §18B-14-11 which is expressly marked repealed.
  Sources: [code.wvlegislature.gov/18B-14-10](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/18B-14-10/) · [full text (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/west-virginia/2010/chapter18b/article14/18B-14-10.html)
  **Practical read for a crypto project in WV:** the statute is written around *credit cards*, so a token/app booth is not squarely within it. But the **no-tangible-gifts-for-signups** norm and the **designated-sites + registration** machinery are exactly the levers a WVU administrator will reach for if your table hands out swag in exchange for an account signup. Assume swag-for-signup is the flagged behavior.

- **GA / NC / SC / VA / TN / KY — NO equivalent campus credit-card marketing statute was located.** This is a **negative finding with a caveat, not a clearance**: the two secondary compendia that enumerate state student-credit-card laws were both unreachable (business.cch.com fails TLS hostname validation; cga.ct.gov robots fetch times out), and a National Law Review treatment of the topic discusses only the **federal** CARD Act / TILA / FCRA layer and names no states at all. So: nothing found, sources partly closed.
  Source: [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/credit-card-marketing-geared-towards-students-and-policies-to-address-dilemma-can)

### 2c. State digital-asset / money-transmitter regimes

None of these seven states has a New York- or Illinois-style regime that bars *unregistered activity outright* in a way that would stop a marketing table. But two of them do reach virtual-currency business activity directly:

| State | Position | Source quality |
|---|---|---|
| **Georgia** | **License required.** The Department of Banking and Finance states plainly: "some providers of virtual currency services must be licensed with the Department to provide services to Georgia consumers." Dept. phone **(770) 986-1633** / **(888) 986-1633** | **PRIMARY** — [dbf.georgia.gov](https://dbf.georgia.gov/money-transmission) |
| **Tennessee** | **Conditional.** "Any company that offers to exchange, administer, or maintain virtual currencies for sovereign currency may be subject to state regulation and licensing," but "Tennessee's Money Transmitter License … do[es] not cover the transmission of virtual currency." TDFI **(615) 741-2236** | **PRIMARY** — [tn.gov/tdfi](https://www.tn.gov/tdfi/mortgage-consumer-lending/money-transmitter/virtual-currency-guidance-.html) |
| **North Carolina** | Money transmission definition **expressly includes virtual currency**; crypto-dealing licensees can face raised surety bonds | SECONDARY — [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) (nccob.gov returned 403) |
| **West Virginia** | Money transmission defined broadly to include "value that substitutes for money"; a **Fintech Regulatory Sandbox** lets participants operate without separate MT licensing | SECONDARY — [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) · [Astraea](https://astraea.law/insights/state-by-state-crypto-licensing-map-2025) |
| **Kentucky** | Statute neither expressly includes nor excludes digital currency; major exchanges hold KY money-services licenses; a kiosk framework is described as incoming | SECONDARY — [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) · [Astraea](https://astraea.law/insights/state-by-state-crypto-licensing-map-2025) |
| **Virginia** | Virtual currency held to fall **outside** money transmission; licensing bites only where a **fiat leg** is involved | SECONDARY — [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) (SCC notice page is robots-disallowed) |
| **South Carolina** | **Most permissive.** The AG's position is that virtual currencies lack the characteristics of a medium of exchange, so **no license is required**; virtual-currency ATMs specifically exempt | SECONDARY — [Bloomberg Law](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/) |

**Net:** the regulatory risk in this region is concentrated in **Georgia** (explicit licensing language, and simultaneously the campus with the best access) and, conditionally, **North Carolina**. **South Carolina is both the cheapest table and the most permissive state** — a notable alignment. Nothing found here resembles a flat bar on unregistered presence. The four SECONDARY rows should be re-verified against the primary regulator before anything transactional happens on a table.
