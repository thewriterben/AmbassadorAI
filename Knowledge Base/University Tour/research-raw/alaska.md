# DGD Fall 2026 Campus Tour Research — ALASKA

## STATE-LEVEL: UA System Policy (report once, applies to campuses 1–3)

The University of Alaska is a single system. Board of Regents policy sits above campus policy, but on this subject **BOR policy is deliberately thin and delegates everything downward** — that is the single most important structural fact for an ambassador.

**Regents' Policy P05.12.100 — Public Use of Facilities** (effective 06-20-97), verbatim:
> "Facilities of the university will be open to the public for educational, recreational, cultural activities, and other use in accordance with use priorities and other requirements as may be set forth in university regulation and campus procedures."

**Regents' Policy P05.12.101 — Campus Solicitation** (effective 06-20-97), verbatim:
> "All canvassing, peddling, or solicitation on university grounds or in university buildings will be subject to university regulation and campus procedures as to time, manner, and place."

Source: https://www.alaska.edu/bor/policy-regulations/chapter-05-12-capital-planning-facilities-management.php

**Practical consequence:** there is no systemwide ban on commercial solicitation and no systemwide fee schedule, insurance limit, or free-speech-zone rule. Both policies punt to "university regulation and campus procedures." Every operative restriction DGD will face is written at the campus level. Do not let anyone tell you "it's a system rule" — ask which campus procedure.

**Regents' Policy Chapter 09.07 — Student Organizations** (https://www.alaska.edu/bor/policy-regulations/chapter-09-07-student-organizations.php):
- **P09.07.030** (04-23-99): "No student organization will be denied registration...on the basis of the views espoused by its members." — viewpoint-neutrality protection; a crypto club cannot be refused recognition for being crypto.
- **P09.07.040.C** (04-23-99): organizations must "avoid any unauthorized representation that they are agents of the university or that their views or actions are attributable to or endorsed" by it; must assume "sole responsibility for their debts and contracts"; and must "use university business offices and practices for financial transactions."
- **R09.07.040** (06-21-99): "Student organizations must sign a license agreement with the university in order to use a university logo, trademark, or servicemark."

The "use university business offices and practices for financial transactions" clause is the closest thing in UA policy to a **payment-credential restriction**. It means a sponsoring club cannot cleanly act as a payment conduit for DGD — money moving through a club must move through university business offices. I found **no** language anywhere in UA policy reaching credit cards, Venmo/payment apps by name, or signing contracts on site.

**Anti-"fronting" language: I found none** at the system level or at UAA. This is a notable absence — many state systems have it. Treat as "not found," not "confirmed absent," for UAF/UAS where I could not read every campus procedure.

**Alaska campus free-speech statute: UNVERIFIED.** I could not confirm on a live page whether Alaska has enacted a campus free-expression statute (the FORUM Act–style laws many states passed 2017–2023). Do not assert one exists. Check Alaska Statutes Title 14 (Education) and confirm by phone with UA General Counsel before relying on any free-speech argument.

## GEOGRAPHY — READ BEFORE PLANNING ANY TRAVEL

This changes whether a physical tour makes sense at all in Alaska:

- **Anchorage → Fairbanks:** ~358 road miles, roughly a 6–7 hour drive on the Parks Highway, in weather that in Sept–Dec ranges from rain to ice. It is a full travel day each way.
- **Juneau is not road-accessible from anywhere.** There is no highway to Juneau from Anchorage, Fairbanks, or the Lower 48. Reaching UAS Juneau requires a flight or the Alaska Marine Highway ferry. A "tour" cannot drive there.
- **APU is the only campus co-located with another** — it sits directly adjacent to UAA in Anchorage (4101 University Drive vs UAA's 3211 Providence Drive). These two are walkable from each other and are the only pairing that shares a trip.

**Recommendation implied by geography:** Alaska is not a tour. It is one Anchorage trip (UAA + APU in the same days), optionally one Fairbanks flight, and Juneau handled remotely. See the summary for the ranked call.

## C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY

**Policy name:** "Use of University Facilities & Property for Non-Academic Purposes," in the UAA Student Handbook section of the Academic Catalog.
**URL:** https://catalog.uaa.alaska.edu/handbook/student-freedoms-rights-and-responsibilities/use-of-university-facilities-and-property-for-non-academic-purposes/
**Effective date:** not stated on the page. The section cites no Board of Regents policy numbers.

**Operative restrictions, verbatim:**

> "Direct advertising, sales and commercial solicitation are not permitted on University property or in University facilities unless under contract with a University College, School, Department, Building Manager, or administrative unit."

> Unaffiliated persons "must be sponsored by a student group/organization or a University school or department in order to use University facilities."

> For outdoor designated speaking areas, unaffiliated groups must "contact the UAA Police Department (UPD) at least ten business days in advance but no more than twelve months in advance."

> "events by University schools or departments and student groups/organizations shall have priority over events and activities conducted by unaffiliated persons."

Fundraising requires advance approval where "monies (directly or indirectly) are exchanged for merchandise, service, entertainment or a chance at winning a prize."

**Analysis — this is the key legal read for the whole state:**

1. **The commercial ban is not absolute; it has an express contract exception.** "unless under contract with a University College, School, Department, Building Manager, or administrative unit." **Paying the $150/$225 Business booth fee at Campus Kickoff IS that contract.** This is the clean, policy-compliant pathway for DGD to be on a UAA quad. It is not a loophole — it is the mechanism the policy itself names.

2. **Sponsorship genuinely cures the access problem at UAA.** The policy says unaffiliated persons "must be sponsored by a student group/organization or a University school or department in order to use University facilities." This is permissive sponsorship language, **not** the "requires approval whether sponsored or not" construction found in stricter systems. A sponsoring student org is a real route.

3. **But sponsorship does not cure the *commercial* restriction.** Read the two clauses together: sponsorship gets an outside entity into a facility; the commercial-solicitation ban is separately gated on a *contract*. A club sponsoring DGD does not by itself authorize "direct advertising, sales and commercial solicitation." An ambassador should be careful not to conflate the two.

4. **Free-speech zone:** UAA runs outdoor "designated speaking areas" for unaffiliated groups, booked through **UAA Police Department**, 10 business days to 12 months ahead. This is a non-commercial channel — useful for expressive activity, not for marketing a financial product.

**Not found / gaps:** no insurance requirement or coverage limit, no deposit or cancellation terms, no explicit ban on financial-product marketing, **no anti-fronting language**, and no language reaching payment credentials or on-site contract signing. I could not load the Event Services contact page or the Student Union reservations detail page (both robots-blocked); the reservations page I did reach says only "competitive rates" with no figures. **These terms almost certainly exist in the Event Space Use Agreement — get it by phone.**
