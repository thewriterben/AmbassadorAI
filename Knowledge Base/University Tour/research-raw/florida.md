## STATE-LEVEL LEGAL LAYER — READ THIS BEFORE ANY CAMPUS SECTION

### 1. Fla. Stat. § 1004.097 — the "Campus Free Expression Act"

This is the single most over-claimed statute an ambassador will encounter in Florida.
It is unusually strong AND it does not help DGD at all. Both things are true.

**What it does:**
- § 1004.097(3)(c): *"Outdoor areas of campus are considered traditional public forums for
  individuals, organizations, and guest speakers."*
- § 1004.097(3)(b): a person may engage in expressive activity in outdoor areas
  *"freely, spontaneously, and contemporaneously as long as the person's conduct is lawful."*
- § 1004.097(4)(a): **an express private cause of action** — *"A person injured by a violation
  of this section may bring an action against a public institution of higher education based
  on the violation of the individual's expressive rights in a court of competent jurisdiction
  to obtain declaratory and injunctive relief and may be entitled to damages plus court costs
  and reasonable attorney fees, which may only be paid from nonstate funds."*
  (For classroom-recording violations there is a separate $200,000 damages cap.)
- Institutions may still impose restrictions that are *"reasonable and content-neutral on time,
  place, and manner"* and *"narrowly tailored to a significant institutional interest,"* which
  must be *"clear and published"* and leave *"ample alternative means of expression."*

**WHY IT DOES NOT REACH DGD — the operative sentence:**
> *"Expressive activities protected by this section do not include defamatory or
> COMMERCIAL SPEECH."* — § 1004.097(2)/(3)(a)

That is dispositive. A cryptocurrency project marketing a token, distributing branded
material, collecting sign-ups or driving wallet installs is engaged in commercial speech.
§ 1004.097 confers **no right whatsoever** on DGD to stand on a Florida public campus.
Every fee, permit, insurance requirement and approval gate documented below is lawful
under it. The statute is a tool for **student** advocates and for non-commercial guest
speakers — it is not a tabling permit.

**Scope:** § 1004.097(2)(e) defines "public institution of higher education" as *"any public
technical center, state college, state university, law school, medical school, dental school,
or other Florida College System institution."* So it reaches **all ten public campuses in this
set including Miami Dade College**, and does NOT reach University of Miami (private).

Sources:
- https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/1004.097
- https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/1004.097
- https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._1004.097

**Do not let an ambassador cite this statute at a table.** It is the fastest way to be
identified as someone who has not read the policy and to lose the room.

### 2. Board of Governors — the hypothesis that one regulation resolves nine campuses is FALSE

I checked the BOG active-regulation index directly. **There is no systemwide Board of
Governors regulation on use of university facilities or on solicitation.** The active list
covers student conduct (6.0105), student affairs administration (6.010), student ombudsman
(6.011), student governments (6.014), hazing (6.021), religious observances (6.0115), equal
access (2.003), restrooms (14.010), opioid antagonists (14.011), building naming (9.005) —
and nothing on facilities use, commercial activity, or solicitation.

Source: https://www.flbog.edu/regulations/active-regulations/
(https://www.flbog.edu/regulations/regulations-active/ returns 404 — dead link.)

The delegation runs the other way. **BOG Regulation 1.001(3)(j)** delegates to each university
board of trustees the authority to adopt its own regulations governing use of facilities and
solicitation — FSU's commercial-solicitation regulation cites exactly that authority in its
implementing note. So the nine State University System campuses each have a **separate**
instrument, and they differ materially: FAU publishes a $46.73 breezeway table rate, FSU
publishes $165 with a product ban that names financial services, FIU flatly bars non-employee
solicitation, UF routes everything through the VP for Business Affairs.

Source (delegation cite visible in FSU-2.013 implementing note):
https://regulations.fsu.edu/sites/g/files/upcbnu486/files/regulations/noticed/20240814/FSU-2.013-Text.pdf

**One BOG instrument does exist and it is neutral:** the *State University System Free
Expression Statement*, adopted by the BOG and its twelve institutions. Its operative closing
language is:
> *"Each institution has adopted regulations that align with Florida's Campus Free Expression
> Act, section 1004.097, Florida Statutes… These limitations are narrowly drawn and
> content-neutral."*

It contains **no commercial-speech provision and no provision on non-affiliated persons**.
Neutral for DGD; do not cite it as authority for anything.
Source: https://www.flbog.edu/bog-fes/

**Miami Dade College is NOT in the State University System.** It is a Florida College System
institution governed by its own District Board of Trustees under § 1001.64, Fla. Stat., and by
the MDC Manual of Procedure. Different rulebook entirely — see the MDC section.

### 3. STATE REGULATORY — Florida money transmission and virtual currency

Florida licenses money transmission under Chapter 560, Fla. Stat., administered by the
**Office of Financial Regulation (OFR)**.

- **CS/HB 273 (2022), effective January 1, 2023**, amended the definition of "money
  transmitter" at **§ 560.103(23)/(24)** to expressly include receipt of *"virtual currency"*
  and to narrow the definition to *"only an intermediary that has the ability to unilaterally
  execute or indefinitely prevent a transaction."*
- **"Virtual currency" is now a defined term: *"a medium of exchange in electronic or digital
  format that is not currency."*** (§ 560.103)
- **"Monetary value"** was amended to mean *"a medium of exchange, other than virtual currency,
  regardless of whether it is redeemable in currency"* — i.e. virtual currency was carved out
  of "monetary value" and given its own hook.
- OFR's own Industry Alert states the agency **changed its interpretation to require licensure
  for two-party virtual-currency transactions**, citing *State v. Espinoza*, 264 So. 3d 1055
  (Fla. 3d DCA 2019) — the Miami Bitcoin-seller prosecution. Selling Bitcoin directly for cash
  can require a Florida money transmitter licence.

**Operational consequence for an ambassador:** do not execute, broker, or facilitate any
token-for-value transaction on a Florida campus. Educational presentation is fine; a
two-party swap at a table is the exact fact pattern OFR named. Custody of a student's funds
or unilateral control over a transaction is the trigger.

- OFR Industry Alert: https://flofr.gov/news/details/industry-alert--change-in-money-transmission-definition
- OFR phone: **(850) 487-9687**
- Statute: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103
- Bill: https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2022/273

## (4) The state campus free-speech statute

**Fla. Stat. § 1004.097 — the Campus Free Expression Act.**
Strong, with an express private cause of action and outdoor areas deemed traditional public
forums — **and completely unavailable to DGD, because *"Expressive activities protected by this
section do not include defamatory or COMMERCIAL SPEECH."*** It reaches all ten public campuses
here including Miami Dade College; it does not reach the University of Miami.

**Three institutions have written the exclusion into their own rules, and the language is worth
carrying:**
- **FAMU Regulation 5.005** (eff. 9-12-2024): expressive activity *"does not… include defamatory
  or commercial speech."*
- **MDC Procedure 1409**: *"Commercial speech includes, but is not limited to, any speech or
  activity in which the individual is engaged in commerce"* and *"Fundraising… is considered
  solicitation and is therefore deemed commercial speech."*
- **UCF-4.029**: use of facilities *"for solicitation or other commercial speech… cannot be
  sponsored by a University organization or University-related organization."*

**There is no BOG regulation on facilities or solicitation.** The BOG's only free-expression
instrument is the *State University System Free Expression Statement*, which is neutral. Each
university legislates for itself under BOG Regulation 1.001(3)(j).

**Anti-fronting is the norm, not the exception — six of eleven have one in writing:**
UCF (*"No organization may reserve University facilities and then permit the facilities to be
used by any other person or organization"*), UF (Policy 4-003, and it captures *"obtaining access
to University students"*), FAU (*"shall not… 'front' for an off-campus group or commercial
vendor"*), USF (MSC, names *"commercial vendor"*), FIU (*"may not reserve an event space or table
for the purposes of/ nor resulting in another entity using the space"*), and UF's Reitz Union fee
adjustment. **Sponsorship by a club is a dead end at UCF, UF, FAU, USF and FIU.** It is
*irrelevant* at FAMU, UNF and USF because a direct paid channel exists.

## (6) THE ONE THING TO KNOW

**Florida looks like an open state and is not the one it looks like.** § 1004.097 gives
outdoor campus areas traditional-public-forum status and hands injured speakers a private cause
of action with attorney's fees — and then excludes commercial speech in a single clause that
takes all of it back from DGD. Miami Dade College's own procedure spells out the reasoning every
Florida administrator will use: *"any speech or activity in which the individual is engaged in
commerce"* is commercial, and *"fundraising… is therefore deemed commercial speech."*

So the access story in Florida is not constitutional, it is commercial: **five campuses sell
tables to outsiders at a published price, and that is the whole game.** FAMU at $45 walk-up,
UNF at $40 with the entire Fall 2026 schedule already posted, USF at $60, FAU at $46.73, UCF at
an unpublished rate. Pay the fee, obey the product-category bans (credit cards at UCF, UNF and
FAU; banking and financial services at FSU), never execute a token-for-value transaction on
Florida soil, and skip the sponsorship shortcut entirely — five of eleven campuses have written
anti-fronting rules and UCF's bars club sponsorship of commercial speech outright.

**And the single best access route in the state is not a table at all: ShellHacks,
September 25–27, 2026, at FIU's Graham Center. Florida's largest hackathon, run by a student
club, with a live corporate sponsor wall. Sponsoring it is a contract with students, not a
solicitation permit — the one door in Florida that campus commercial rules do not reach.**
