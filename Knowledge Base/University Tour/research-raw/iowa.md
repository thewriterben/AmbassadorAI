## STATE-LEVEL FINDINGS (read before any campus section)

### 1. Iowa Code chapter 261H — the campus free-speech statute. IT EXCLUDES COMMERCIAL SPEECH.

Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/261H.pdf

Confirmed. The chapter exists and is in force. The operative points for an ambassador:

- **261H.3(1)** protects **"noncommercial expressive activities"** — verbatim: *"Noncommercial
  expressive activities protected under the provisions of this chapter include but are not
  limited to any lawful oral or written means by which members of the campus community may
  communicate ideas to one another."* **Commercial speech is outside the chapter entirely.**
- **261H.4(1)**: *"The outdoor areas of campuses of public institutions of higher education
  in this state shall be deemed public forums"*, subject to *"viewpoint-neutral time, place,
  and manner restrictions"*, and institutions must *"allow members of the campus community
  to engage in spontaneous expressive activity and to distribute literature."*
- **261H.1(2)** defines "campus community" to include *"guests invited to a public
  institution of higher education by the institution's students, administrators, faculty,
  or staff."* DGD is not a member of the campus community; it can only ever be an
  **invited guest**, and the invitation has to come from inside.
- **261H.3(3)** — the unusual student-organization provision: institutions *"shall not deny
  any benefit or privilege to a student organization based on the student organization's
  requirement that the leaders of the student organization agree to and support the student
  organization's beliefs, as those beliefs are interpreted and applied by the
  organization."* This is a **belief-based leadership-criteria protection**, not a viewpoint
  clause — it protects a club's right to require its officers to hold the club's beliefs.
  It is genuinely useful to a student club being pressured over its membership rules. It
  does **nothing** for an outside for-profit.

**Bottom line: 261H is a tool for student advocates, not a right to table.** Every approval
requirement, fee and vendor tier in this packet is lawful under it. Do not let an
ambassador cite it at a table.

Binds the three Regent universities (Iowa, Iowa State, UNI). Whether the chapter's
definition of "public institution of higher education" reaches Iowa's community colleges
(and therefore DMACC) was **not verified** — check 261H.1 before relying on it. It does
**not** bind Drake, Grinnell or Coe, which are private.

### 3. ⚠⚠ IAC 681 ch. 11–14 RESCINDED IN FULL — EFFECTIVE TODAY, AUGUST 12, 2026

This is the single most time-sensitive legal fact in the state, and it is dated to the day
this research was done.

Sources:
- https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/681.13.pdf
- https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/aco/arc/0427D.pdf
- https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/agency/08-12-2026.681.pdf

Iowa Administrative Code agency 681 (Board of Regents), chapters **11** (Board of Regents
Organization and General Rules), **12** (University of Iowa), **13** (Iowa State University)
and **14** (University of Northern Iowa) were each **"rescinded in its entirety" with
"nothing" replacing them** — ARC 0424D, 0425D, 0426D and 0427D respectively, IAB 7/8/26,
**effective August 12, 2026**. The Board adopted the rescissions June 16, 2026; two public
hearings (April 28 and April 30, 2026) drew no attendees and no comments. The stated reason
is housekeeping: *"Pursuant to 2024 Iowa Acts, Senate File 2370"* (signed May 17, 2024),
agencies no longer must include *"a description of their organization, general course and
method of operations and a statement of mission in the Iowa Administrative Code."* The
current agency-681 listing now shows **Chapters 6, 8, 11–16: Reserved.**

**What was in them, verbatim (captured from the 12-11-2024 edition before rescission):**

- **681—13.15(262) Commercial and Charitable Uses** (ISU): *"Commercial solicitation,
  advertising and sales are not permitted on the campus except as follows"* — newspapers in
  established locations; bulletin-board advertising per 13.13(1); and *"Commercial sales or
  solicitation may be approved by the senior vice president for operations and finance"*,
  which may be granted in academic areas only if directly related to programs, otherwise
  *"only in the area directly to the north of the Memorial Union, with priority being given
  to all other campus-related uses."*
  Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/12-11-2024.681.13.pdf
- **681—14.2(1)** (UNI): *"Sales persons or agents for any product, proposition, or cause
  are prohibited from soliciting employees or students in any building or part of the
  university property, except with the permission of the vice president for finance and
  operations in the case of employees or the vice president for student affairs in the case
  of the students."*
  Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/12-11-2024.681.14.pdf
- **Chapter 12 (University of Iowa)** contained **no** solicitation, commercial-activity or
  facilities-use rule at all — mission, officers, operations manual, contracting authority,
  tobacco, alcohol, communications, merit grievances only.

**What this means, and what it does NOT mean:**

- **UNI:** no operational change. UNI restates the identical prohibition in campus policy
  **8.07**, which is independent of the Code. The written rule still binds.
- **Iowa State:** no operational change worth banking on. The Memorial Union Facility Use
  Policies restate the substance and add more. Do not argue that ISU lost its solicitation
  rule today.
- **University of Iowa:** a genuine oddity. UI's Operations Manual **V-35.6(4)(g)** bans
  commercial solicitation *"except as explicitly permitted by IAC (681)13.15"* — a
  cross-reference to (a) **Iowa State's** rule, not UI's own, and (b) as of today, a rule
  that **no longer exists**. **The prohibition survives; only the exception's referent is
  now a dangling citation.** Read honestly, that makes UI's campus-wide commercial ban
  *harder*, not softer — the only named escape hatch has been repealed, leaving the IMU
  student-organization sponsorship route as the operative path. ⚠ **DO NOT let an
  ambassador spin the rescission as deregulation.**

### 4. Iowa Code chapter 715D — Iowa DOES have a comprehensive consumer privacy act

Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/715d.pdf

Chapter **715D "Consumer Data Protections"**, enacted **2023 Acts, ch 17**, is codified and
in the current Iowa Code. The working premise that Iowa has none is wrong. What matters:

- **"Consumer"** = *"A natural person who is a resident of the state acting only in an
  individual or household context and excluding a natural person acting in a commercial or
  employment context."*
- **Thresholds:** controls or processes personal data of **at least 100,000** Iowa consumers
  in a year; **or** at least 25,000 consumers **and** derives **over 50% of gross revenue**
  from selling personal data.
- **Exempt entities include institutions of higher education**, the state and its political
  subdivisions, GLBA financial institutions, HIPAA entities, and nonprofits.
- Sensitive data requires only **clear notice plus an opt-out** — *not* opt-in consent
  (unusual; most state acts require opt-in). COPPA governs children's data.
- **715D.9** preempts city, county, municipal and local-agency data rules. Enforcement is
  **exclusive to the Attorney General**.

**Practical read:** a campus signup sheet or wallet-download list will not come within an
order of magnitude of the thresholds, and the campuses themselves are exempt. 715D is not a
constraint on this tour. Note: the chapter text as fetched does not print an effective-date
section; the delayed operative date should be confirmed if it ever matters.

## 4. The state's campus free-speech statute

**Iowa Code chapter 261H exists, and it does not help.** 261H.3(1) protects only
**"noncommercial expressive activities."** 261H.4(1) deems the outdoor areas of **public**
campuses public forums, but only for the **"campus community"**, which 261H.1(2) defines to
include *"guests invited to a public institution of higher education by the institution's
students, administrators, faculty, or staff"* — DGD can only ever be an invited guest, and
the invitation must originate inside. Board of Regents Policy 4.2 repeats both filters
verbatim. The statute's distinctive provision — 261H.3(3), barring denial of benefits to a
student organization because it *"require[s] that the leaders of the student organization
agree to and support the student organization's beliefs"* — is a genuine protection for
student clubs and is worth knowing when helping a club form, but it confers nothing on an
outside company. Binds Iowa, Iowa State and UNI (and possibly DMACC — unverified). Does not
bind Drake, Grinnell or Coe.

**Companion state facts:** Board of Regents Policy 4.14 is a single sentence about student
organizations and says nothing about outside entities; **IAC 681 chapters 11–14 were
rescinded in their entirety effective TODAY, August 12, 2026** (ARC 0424D–0427D, IAB
7/8/26), as housekeeping under 2024 Iowa Acts SF 2370 — the campus policies restating those
rules survive, and UI's Operations Manual is now left citing a repealed rule; and **Iowa
Code ch. 715D "Consumer Data Protections" is on the books** but exempts institutions of
higher education, sets thresholds (100,000 Iowa consumers, or 25,000 plus >50% of revenue
from data sales) far above anything this tour will generate, and requires only notice and
opt-out for sensitive data.
