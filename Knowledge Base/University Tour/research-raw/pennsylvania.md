## STATEWIDE CONTEXT — READ THIS BEFORE ANY CAMPUS SECTION

### 1. Three distinct governance families. Conflating them produces wrong answers.

**(a) State-related universities — Penn State, Pitt, Temple (and Lincoln).** These are NOT
state-owned. They are privately chartered, privately governed corporations designated
"state-related" instrumentalities of the Commonwealth, receiving a non-preferred
appropriation. They write their own policy with no system-level board above them. There is
no shared policy layer between Penn State's AD-series, Pitt's Student Unions handbook and
Temple's Policies and Procedures Manual — all three are separate and materially different.
For First Amendment purposes, the Third Circuit held in **Krynicky v. University of
Pittsburgh, 742 F.2d 94 (3d Cir. 1984)** that Pitt and Temple ARE state actors under §1983
by virtue of the Commonwealth Act relationship
(https://www.leagle.com/decision/1984836742f2d941825 ·
https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/560/803/1481871/). Penn State's
state-actor status is less settled and was not resolved on any page retrieved. Practical
effect for DGD: **none.** Public-forum doctrine protects non-commercial expression; DGD's
activity is commercial and sits outside it at every one of these three.

**(b) PASSHE — Pennsylvania's State System of Higher Education.** Binds **West Chester
University** and **Indiana University of Pennsylvania** in this set. These are genuinely
state-owned, and a PASSHE Board of Governors policy layer sits above each campus
(https://www.passhe.edu/policies/). ⚠ **I could not retrieve a PASSHE Board of Governors
policy governing facility use, solicitation or non-university groups.** Searching PASSHE's
policy library surfaced only unrelated items (e.g. Technology Transfer and
Commercialization Services 2018-37,
https://www.passhe.edu/policies/documents/Policies_Procedures_Standards/Technology%20Transfer%20and%20Commercialization%20Services%202018-37.pdf).
Treat the campus-level policy as operative at WCU and IUP and say so; do not assert a BOG
policy exists or does not exist. **GAP — close by phone.**

**(c) Fully private institutions — Penn, CMU, Drexel, Villanova, Duquesne.** No public-forum
obligation whatsoever. Villanova and Duquesne are additionally Catholic (Augustinian and
Spiritan respectively), which adds a mission-compatibility screen on top of the commercial
screen. Capture this honestly: at these five, "we have a First Amendment right to be here"
is simply false and saying it will end the conversation.

**Community College of Philadelphia** is a twelfth category — a public two-year college
sponsored by the City of Philadelphia, governed by its own Board of Trustees, outside both
PASSHE and the state-related family.

### 2. ⚠⚠ THERE IS NO PENNSYLVANIA CAMPUS FREE-EXPRESSION STATUTE.

Pennsylvania has **not** enacted a Chicago-Statement-style campus free-speech act, has no
statutory ban on free-speech zones, and has no statutory forum right for outside speakers.
FIRE's own report on the First Amendment responsibilities of Pennsylvania state-funded
colleges grounds the obligation entirely in **the First Amendment and Sections 3 and 7 of
the Pennsylvania Constitution** (freedom of conscience and freedom of speech), citing no
state campus-speech statute at all
(https://www.fire.org/research-learn/fire-report-first-amendment-responsibilities-pennsylvania-state-funded-colleges-and).

**Operative point: even if such a statute existed, it would not reach COMMERCIAL speech.**
Every state campus-speech act in the country protects "expressive activity" and either
excludes commercial speech expressly or is read that way. Pennsylvania gives DGD nothing.
An ambassador should never invoke free speech at a Pennsylvania table — it is legally empty
here and it signals to the staffer that you have not read the policy.

### 3. ⚠⚠ THE STATUTE THAT DOES MATTER: 24 P.S. § 23-2302-A — CREDIT CARD MARKETING.

Pennsylvania **does** have a higher-education statute that reaches payment credentials
directly. Act of July 15, 2004, P.L. 722, No. 82, amending the Public School Code of 1949,
codified at **24 P.S. § 23-2302-A, "Regulation of on-campus credit card marketing"**:

> "The board shall require an institution of higher education to establish a policy that
> regulates the marketing of credit cards on campus."

The statute permits an institution's policy to **"prohibit any marketing of credit cards on
the campus"** outright, and directs institutions to consider (1) registering on-campus credit
card marketers; (2) limiting marketers to designated campus areas; (3) prohibiting gifts in
exchange for credit card applications unless debt-education materials are also provided;
(4) distributing debt-education literature with bookstore purchases. As amended, for
students entering in 2024-2025 and after, orientation must include "presentations on credit
card debt education and money management skills."
Text: https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-24-ps-education/pa-st-sect-24-23-2302-a/
Session law: https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/HTM/2004/0/0082..HTM

**This is why three separate Pennsylvania campuses in this set carry an explicit
credit-card-marketing ban, and it is the closest thing in the state to a rule reaching
payment credentials:**
- **West Chester** vendor page: "Credit card marketing is prohibited per Pennsylvania School
  Code Section 2302-A." (https://www.wcupa.edu/_services/stu.syk/vendorinfo.aspx)
- **CCP** Memorandum #158: "The marketing of credit cards on campus or via College-provided
  communication systems is prohibited."
  (https://myccp.online/college-policies/solicitation-campus-facilities)
- **CMU** Cohon University Center: "The marketing and/or solicitation of credit cards is
  strictly prohibited on Carnegie Mellon's campus or at any approved tabling or university
  event." (https://www.cmu.edu/cohon-university-center/policies/index.html)

The statute names credit cards, not crypto wallets or payment apps. But a risk-averse
student-affairs officer looking at a table where students are being walked through
onboarding a wallet and linking a funding source **will reach for this rule**, and at CMU,
WCU and CCP the rule is already written in absolute terms. Do not put a card reader, a
Venmo/CashApp QR, or a "link your bank" flow on a Pennsylvania table. Assume the ban
applies and design the table so it obviously does not.

### 4. Pennsylvania money-transmitter treatment of virtual currency — and the 2024 reversal.

The **Pennsylvania Department of Banking and Securities** issued guidance in **January 2019**
concluding that virtual currency is **not "money"** under the Pennsylvania Money Transmitter
Act (7 P.S. § 6101 et seq.), so virtual-currency exchange platforms, kiosks/ATMs and vending
machines did **not** require a money transmitter licence:
- https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2019/01/25/pa-department-of-banking-and-securities-virtual-currency-is-not-money/
- https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2019/2/pennsylvania-regulator-deems-virtual-currency-trading-platforms-.html
- https://www.hunton.com/blockchain-legal-resource/pennsylvania-issues-money-transmitter-act-guidance-virtual-currency-businesses
- https://natlawreview.com/article/pa-department-banking-and-securities-virtual-currency-not-money

⚠ **In 2024 the Department reversed that position**, reinterpreting existing law to treat
virtual currency **as "money"** under the Money Transmitter Act — a change made without
rulemaking:
- https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/05/13/pa-department-of-banking-and-securities-virtual-currency-is-money/
- https://www.moneylaunderingnews.com/2024/05/pa-department-of-banking-and-securities-virtual-currency-is-money/
- https://www.mcglinchey.com/insights/pennsylvania-quietly-reinterprets-current-law-to-regulate-virtual-currency-as-money-under-the-money-transmitter-act/
  (⚠ this page returned a server error to research tooling; headline and framing confirmed
  from the search index and from the two law-firm blogs above, which independently report the
  same reversal)

**Status: PARTIAL.** The reversal is corroborated by three independent law-firm publications,
but the Department's own primary guidance document was not retrieved. **Do not quote a
specific sentence of the DoBS guidance to a university official.** The operative point for
an ambassador is directional and it is important: **Pennsylvania today treats virtual
currency as money for money-transmission purposes.** Anything at a table that looks like
receiving, holding or transmitting value on a student's behalf is squarely in the
Department's newly claimed perimeter. Educational content, wallet self-custody walkthroughs
and airdrops handled entirely by the student on the student's own device are a different
posture from anything DGD custodies. Get this cleared by counsel before the tour, not after.
DoBS: https://www.dobs.pa.gov/

### 5. Geography is the entire planning story: two clusters, nothing in between.

**Philadelphia cluster — five of the eleven campuses inside roughly 40 minutes:** Temple
(North Broad), Drexel and Penn (University City, literally adjacent — Drexel's campus and
Penn's campus touch at 34th/Market), Community College of Philadelphia (Spring Garden, ~2
miles from Temple), and Villanova (Main Line, ~35–40 min out the Route 100 / I-76 corridor).
All five are reachable on SEPTA. A single Philadelphia week can hit five campuses.

**Pittsburgh cluster — three campuses inside roughly 15 minutes:** Pitt and CMU are
contiguous in Oakland (Forbes Avenue runs through both; walking distance, ~15 minutes end to
end), and Duquesne sits on the Bluff downtown, ~10 minutes from Oakland. A single Pittsburgh
day can hit all three.

**The other three are isolated.** University Park is ~3.5 hours from Philadelphia and ~2.5
hours from Pittsburgh, in the geographic centre of the state with nothing near it. West
Chester is ~35 minutes west of Philadelphia (arguably a Philadelphia-cluster satellite).
Indiana, PA is ~1 hour east of Pittsburgh in rural Indiana County.

Practical shape of a Pennsylvania tour: **one Philadelphia block, one Pittsburgh block, one
dedicated University Park trip built around HackPSU (Oct 24), and IUP only as a bolt-on to
the Pittsburgh block.**

### 6. ⚠ Drexel is on QUARTERS and will not line up with anything else in the state.

Drexel's Fall **quarter** begins **Tuesday, September 22, 2026** — **four weeks after** the
Aug 24 wave and two weeks after CCP. Drexel students are still on co-op or on summer break
through the entire first month of everyone else's semester. A tour built on semester dates
misses Drexel completely. Drexel also runs a **co-op calendar** on top of the quarters, which
means a large share of the undergraduate population is off campus working full-time in any
given term rather than in class. See the Drexel section for the transition note — Drexel
converts to semesters on **August 23, 2027**, which is a Fall 2027 problem, not a Fall 2026
one.

### C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY
Penn State has a numbered Administrative Policy series and the relevant policies are AD27,
AD02 and AD51. **AD27 is the one that closes the door.**

**⚠⚠ AD27 — Commercial Sales Activities at University Locations.** Effective August 14, 1981;
most recent substantive changes December 2, 1996. https://policy.psu.edu/policies/ad27

> **"THE USE OF UNIVERSITY FACILITIES AND/OR PROPERTY FOR COMMERCIAL SALES ACTIVITIES BY
> INDIVIDUALS OR NON-UNIVERSITY ORGANIZATIONS IS PROHIBITED."**

No individual may "use University facilities in the conduct of a commercial enterprise for
his/her own benefit." Sales may not disturb residence halls, interrupt classes, or conflict
with University operations. University organizations may sell materials supporting their own
purposes provided members conduct the activity and all funds go to the organization.
Exceptions are narrow: commercial products may be demonstrated or purchased **within**
University-sponsored lectures, concerts, exhibits or displays; sampling is permitted at
hotels, unions, dining halls and sports venues (no alcohol or tobacco). Scope excludes the
Nittany Lion Inn, the Penn Stater Conference Center Hotel, Beaver Stadium, Rec Hall and the
Bryce Jordan Center — those are separately governed rental venues.

**AD02 — Non-University Groups Using University Facilities.** Effective October 17, 1994;
updated October 18, 2022. https://policy.psu.edu/policies/ad02

Scope: "Non-University groups such as organized civic, cultural, service, religious, and
industrial organizations, public school groups, and college and university groups."
Operative conditions, verbatim where retrievable:
> "University use of all facilities shall have absolute preemptive priority over all
> non-University groups."
> **"The use of the facility must be for noncommercial purposes"** (cross-referencing AD27).
> "Any use of the name of the University … must be approved in advance by the University."
> "The University reserves the right to require insurance coverage" and may require the
> presence of University Safety personnel. **No dollar limit is published in AD02.**
> Groups must sign a "University Release and Indemnification Agreement."
> "The University may charge a fee for the use of its facilities provided that all provisions
> of Policy FN27 are met."

**Sponsorship / anti-fronting.** At University Park, fund-raising activity "must be conducted
in conjunction with, or co-sponsored by, an organized student group, University college,
department or administrative unit" — **and the co-sponsorship must be with "a bona fide
parallel-interest organization with equal participation in the planning and implementation of
the activity."** That "bona fide … equal participation" clause is Penn State's anti-fronting
rule in substance: a club cannot simply put its name on DGD's table. Note also that funds
raised must go to a tax-exempt organization. **Approval authority at University Park:
Strategic Communications** (elsewhere: the campus budget executive).

**AD51 — Use of Outdoor Areas for Expressive Activities.** Effective August 7, 2008; most
recent comprehensive changes June 3, 2024. https://policy.psu.edu/policies/ad51
> "Penn State students, faculty, staff, and registered University student organizations or a
> group of ten or more University students, faculty, or staff may reserve the University Park
> campus outdoor areas."
> Applies to **"noncommercial activities (generally referred to as expressive activity)"** —
> cross-referencing AD26 and AD27 for anything commercial.
> "Reservations must be requested at least fourteen business days in advance of the proposed
> activity" and "can only be made for one location per day and the same location cannot be
> reserved for more than one day in each calendar week."
> External parties are told to "direct questions and requests to Campus and Community Events
> in the Office of Strategic Communications."

Companion: **AD02A Supplemental Policy for Speeches, Rallies, and Demonstrations**
(https://policy.psu.edu/ad02a-supplemental-policy-speeches-rallies-and-demonstrations) and
**AD26 Sale and Serving of Food and Beverages** (effective June 19, 2013,
https://policy.psu.edu/policies/ad26 — food/beverage only; Pepsi exclusivity; irrelevant to
DGD except that you cannot hand out branded drinks).

**⚠⚠ TWO THINGS AN AMBASSADOR MUST INTERNALISE ABOUT DREXEL:**

**(1) The quarter calendar.** Drexel students are **not on campus** during the last week of
August or the first three weeks of September. If you plan a Philadelphia swing timed to
Temple's, Penn's or CCP's start, Drexel's campus will be near-empty. Drexel's high-density
window is **late September through early December** — precisely the window when Pitt (ends
Dec 4) and Penn State (Thanksgiving Nov 22) are winding down. Drexel is the **last** stop of a
Pennsylvania tour, not the first.

the undergraduate body is on a full-time six-month co-op placement in any given term rather
than in class. https://drexel.edu/scdc/about/semesters-and-co-op ·

## (4) THE STATE'S CAMPUS FREE-SPEECH STATUTE

**⚠⚠ THERE ISN'T ONE.** Pennsylvania has not enacted a campus free-expression act. Public and
state-funded institutions are bound by the **First Amendment** and by **Sections 3 and 7 of
the Pennsylvania Constitution**, and by nothing more specific
(https://www.fire.org/research-learn/fire-report-first-amendment-responsibilities-pennsylvania-state-funded-colleges-and).
Even if a statute existed it would protect *expressive*, non-commercial activity — the
boundary that matters, and the one DGD sits on the wrong side of at every campus. **Never
raise free speech at a Pennsylvania table.**

Two constitutional footnotes that are true but not useful to an ambassador: (a) the Third
Circuit held in **Krynicky v. University of Pittsburgh, 742 F.2d 94 (3d Cir. 1984)** that Pitt
and Temple are state actors, so their policies are in principle subject to First Amendment
scrutiny; (b) PASSHE campuses (West Chester, IUP) are unambiguously state actors. Neither
fact creates a commercial right of access. The five private campuses — Penn, CMU, Drexel,
Villanova, Duquesne — have no public-forum obligation at all.

**⚠ The Pennsylvania statute that DOES bite is 24 P.S. § 23-2302-A** (Act of July 15, 2004,
P.L. 722, No. 82), "Regulation of on-campus credit card marketing," which requires every
Pennsylvania institution of higher education to adopt a policy regulating credit-card
marketing on campus and expressly permits that policy to "prohibit any marketing of credit
cards on the campus." Three campuses in this set implement it as a flat ban — **CMU** ("The
marketing and/or solicitation of credit cards is strictly prohibited… or at any approved
tabling or university event"), **West Chester** ("Credit card marketing is prohibited per
Pennsylvania School Code Section 2302-A"), and **CCP** ("The marketing of credit cards on
campus or via College-provided communication systems is prohibited"). Assume every
Pennsylvania campus has such a policy even where it was not retrieved. **Keep payment
credentials off the table entirely.**

**⚠ Money transmission:** the PA Department of Banking and Securities said in **January 2019**
that virtual currency is **not "money"** under the PA Money Transmitter Act, then **reversed in
2024** and now treats virtual currency **as "money."** Corroborated by three independent
law-firm publications; **the Department's primary guidance document was not retrieved —
PARTIAL.** Do not quote it verbatim to anyone. Do brief counsel before the tour.

## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION

Pennsylvania splits cleanly into **two clusters and one outlier**: five campuses in
Philadelphia within 40 minutes (Temple, Drexel, Penn, Villanova, CCP — plus West Chester 35
minutes further), three in Pittsburgh within 15 minutes (Pitt, CMU, Duquesne), and Penn State
alone in the middle of the state. **The written policy, not the audience, decides where to
go.** Temple ($70/table) and West Chester ($60/day) publish for-profit vendor rates and will
simply sell you a table; Pitt rents an information table year-round; Penn sells leftover
dates with $2M insurance. Penn State — the largest audience in the state — flatly prohibits
commercial sales by non-University organisations under AD27, and Duquesne prohibits
non-affiliates from even distributing literature under TAP 37. **CMU has the best technical
audience in Pennsylvania and one of its tightest doors, including the only explicit ban on
credit-card marketing "at any approved tabling or university event" — which, together with
West Chester's and CCP's identical bans and the statute behind them (24 P.S. § 23-2302-A),
means no payment credentials on any Pennsylvania table, ever.** There is no Pennsylvania
campus free-speech statute to invoke, and it would not reach commercial speech if there were.
**The two doors that actually beat the policy regime are private student events: HackPSU Fall
2026 (Sat Oct 24, published tiers $1,500–$8,000, sponsorship@hackpsu.org) and SteelHacks XIII
at Pitt (Sep 19–20). The best single human being in the state is Kevin Werbach at Wharton —
(215) 898-1222 — who directs the Blockchain and Digital Asset Project, teaches both the
undergraduate and MBA crypto courses, and advises the club that runs the largest student crypto
conference in the region.** And **Drexel is on quarters: it does not start until September 22
and its students are on co-op — build it into the end of the tour or miss it entirely.**
