# Focused Sweep — Midwest 6: Crypto/Blockchain Student Clubs × Tabling Access

**Scope:** Michigan, Indiana, Kansas, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota
**Date of sweep:** August 12, 2026
**Question:** where does an ACTIVE crypto club coexist with a fast — ideally *purchasable* — route for an outside crypto project to get a table this fall?

**Headline:** the sweep produced a clean winner. **The University of Kansas prints a $20/day table rate for an outside entity sponsored by a campus organization** (versus $200/day unsponsored), and KU has the single best-verified active crypto club in all six states — the KU Blockchain Institute pushed code to its own website repository on **Aug 2, 2026**, ten days before this sweep. KU classes begin **Mon Aug 24, 2026**. Second is **Western Michigan**, which charges outside vendors a flat **$50** for the Bernhard Center solicitation table and lists a Blockchain Crypto Club in its current org directory. The two flagships with the loudest clubs — U-M Ann Arbor and IU Bloomington — both sit behind explicit anti-commercial-solicitation rules.

**Not found anywhere in these six states:** a state statute banning credit-card or financial-product marketing to students. The Minnesota/Pennsylvania/West Virginia/New Jersey pattern does **not** repeat here. Federal Reg Z §1026.57(c) still applies in all six.

## 1. TOP PICKS

### #1 — University of Kansas (Lawrence) — the only clean intersection in six states
This is the strongest single target in the sweep and it is not close.
- **The club is provably alive right now.** Not a live-looking website — **actual commits**. The KU Blockchain Institute's GitHub org shows `kubi-website` updated **Aug 2, 2026**, `dao-docs` Jun 28, 2026, `hack` Mar 23, 2026, plus attendance at **ETHDenver 2026**. Two independent working email addresses are published (`BlockChalk@kublockchain.com` on the org profile; `KU.blockchain.institute@gmail.com` on the university's own Rock Chalk Central page), plus an open Discord.
- **Access is a printed price, not a negotiation.** **$20 per day** for an outside entity sponsored by a campus organization; **$200 per day** unsponsored. A ten-to-one discount for having a sponsor is effectively KU telling you to go get the club to sign. Which is exactly the play: email KUBI, get them to sponsor, pay $20.
- **The sponsor requirement is a feature here.** "Non-KU groups must be sponsored by a university department or registered student organization" — and there is an active, reachable, contact-rich crypto RSO on campus willing to be that sponsor. That is the whole thesis of this sweep in one campus.
- **Timing is the pressure point.** Classes begin **Mon Aug 24, 2026** — twelve days out. Fees are **non-refundable and payable in advance**, and off-campus vendors are capped at **5 days per semester**, so pick the days deliberately.
- **One call does it:** KU Memorial Union Event Services, **785-864-1170**, eventservices@ku.edu.
- **Watch:** if the KU name or Jayhawk logo appears on anything, permission from the University Trademark Licensing Administrator is required. And vendors may not leave the assigned 5'×6' footprint to approach passers-by — no roaming.

## 2. BLOCKERS

**Hard campus blockers**
1. **IU Bloomington — Policy FIN-PURCH-12.** Bans "Commercial solicitations (including door-to-door solicitation, chalking, distributing or affixing any kind of written or printed commercial materials, sales of goods or services)" by entities "not affiliated with the university." The tabling form confirms the workaround is not a workaround: "Tabling involving outside businesses requires approval from IU Purchasing."
2. **U-M Ann Arbor — Grounds Guideline (doc dated July 6, 2026).** "In general, sales are prohibited on University of Michigan grounds." Non-affiliated sponsored orgs need a VPFO agreement in place beforehand; 30-day advance Outdoor Event Request Form. Plus the Unions rule: "non-university groups cannot solicit sales from the University community without the Center for Campus Involvement approval." U-M Housing tables are RSO/department-only outright.
3. **Purdue Northwest — total commercial-gain ban.** "Use of university facilities in any manner which results in financial gain, either directly or indirectly, to individuals or non-university approved organizations or groups is prohibited." Nothing to buy.
4. **UNL — RSO-only booths.** The Union booth/plaza-table product is reserved to Recognized Student Organizations. External groups are not shut out of campus (a Non-Affiliated rate column exists) but are pushed into room/plaza rentals at **2× the affiliated rate plus a 50% non-university scheduling fee** — i.e. a $110 affiliated plaza slot becomes roughly $330 all-in for you. Expensive relative to KU's $20.
5. **KU — operational, not prohibitive.** 5 days per semester cap for off-campus vendors; fees prepaid and non-refundable; no roaming beyond the assigned 5'×6'; trademark permission required to use KU marks.

**Credit-card / financial-product marketing to students**
- **NO state statute found in Michigan, Indiana, Kansas, Nebraska, North Dakota, or South Dakota.** Two targeted searches for state-level campus credit-card marketing statutes surfaced only institutional policies (all from New York CUNY/SUNY and private schools) and federal law. The Minnesota §135A.145 / Pennsylvania 24 P.S. §23-2302-A / West Virginia §18B-14-10 / N.J.S.A. 18A:3C-1 pattern **does not extend into these six states** on the evidence gathered. Caveat: this is a negative from search, not a statute-by-statute code review — treat as "none found," not "none exists."
- **Federal floor applies in all six anyway — Reg Z, 12 CFR §1026.57(c).** "No card issuer or creditor may offer a college student any tangible item to induce such student to apply for or open an open-end consumer credit plan" when offered **on campus**, **near campus** (official commentary: within **1,000 feet** of the campus border), or **at an event sponsored by or related to** the institution. **Direct operational consequence:** if the table's offer involves a credit product, a card, or anything with a credit line attached, the swag bowl becomes a legal problem. Merch-for-signup at the table is the specific thing this rule prohibits.

**State digital-asset / money-transmitter exposure** (source: Astraea Counsel state-by-state crypto licensing map, 2026 — **single secondary source, verify with each regulator before relying**)
- **Michigan — license required.** DIFS; MCL 487.1001 et seq.
- **Indiana — conditional.** Licensing triggered "where a transaction involves a fiat-currency leg." DFI; IC 28-8-4.1.
- **Kansas — conditional.** Turns on third-party fiat involvement. OSBC; K.S.A. 9-508 et seq.
- **Nebraska — license required**, plus a distinctive **digital-asset depository charter** regime. Neb. Rev. Stat. ch. 8, art. 27.
- **North Dakota — license required**, with a **crypto-kiosk sub-regime**. N.D.C.C. ch. 13-09.1.
- **South Dakota — license required by regulator interpretation.** SDCL ch. 51A-17.
- **No bespoke BitLicense/DACPA-style regime exists in any of the six.** None of these states has New York's or Illinois's dedicated digital-asset licensing statute. The practical read: a purely educational table is low-risk everywhere here; a table that transmits value, sells tokens, or onboards custody/exchange/wallet accounts pulls the state money-transmission analysis in — hardest in **Nebraska** (depository charter regime) and **Michigan** (unconditional MT requirement), softest in **Indiana** and **Kansas** (no fiat leg, arguably no license).

**Rules reaching payment credentials / sign-ups / on-site agreements**
- **Nothing state-level found in these six states.** No rule surfaced barring app sign-ups, collection of payment credentials, or execution of agreements at a campus table.
- The binding constraints are the general ones: Reg Z §1026.57(c) on credit-product inducements, and campus-level solicitation rules (IU, U-M, PNW) that reach "sales of goods or services" broadly enough to cover an on-site sign-up flow.
