## 1. TOP PICKS

### #1 — UConn (Storrs, CT) — the only clean intersection in nine states
The only campus where **an active crypto club and a published, purchasable table rate coexist**.
- **Club is live now:** full exec board seated, weekly newsletter and blog, presentations/guest speakers, field trips, active Instagram. The club's own page tells prospective members to "reach out at the start of the semester" — i.e. it is running a fall recruitment cycle.
- **Access is priced, not negotiated:** **$100.00 for a six-foot indoor table** for non-affiliated organizations. $200 buys outdoor vending on Fairfield Way, the main pedestrian artery.
- **Requirements are ordinary and pre-known:** signed Facilities Use Agreement, $1M certificate of liability insurance naming UConn and its Board of Trustees as Additional Insured, daytime M–F. No mystery approvals.
- **Term starts Mon Aug 31, 2026** — the earliest confirmed start of the three known, so it is also the first door that opens.
- **One caveat, and it is real:** see BLOCKERS. Connecticut's money-transmission regime expressly reaches *advertising* money transmission services. Table content must stay educational; do not advertise custody, transfer, exchange or wallet services at a CT table without counsel. Also note UConn's own rule that credit-card vending is confined to the Student Union and requires registration plus distribution of credit-management education materials.

## 2. BLOCKERS

### A. Connecticut — money transmission, and it reaches *advertising* (HIGHEST-IMPACT FINDING)
**C.G.S. §§ 36a-595 to 36a-612.** The CT Department of Banking states licensure is triggered by holding virtual or fiat currency on behalf of customers, transmitting it on behalf of others, **and by "advertising money transmission services."** Virtual-currency kiosks were folded in effective **Oct 1, 2024** (Public Act No. 24-146). An exchange that merely matches buyer and seller through software without holding or transmitting does not require licensure.
**Why it matters here:** advertising is itself an enumerated trigger. A CT table that promotes a custodial wallet, exchange or transfer service risks being unlicensed advertising of money transmission. This directly qualifies the #1 pick. The DOB page does **not** discuss any exemption for promotional or educational activity.

### B. New Jersey — statutory campus credit-card regime **CONFIRMED**
**N.J.S.A. 18A:3C-1 to 18A:3C-3 (L.2009, c.148).**
- **18A:3C-1** defines "solicit" as *any* on-campus activity "specifically intended, in whole or in part, to encourage, entice, urge or request that a student apply for a credit card." "Credit card" is broadly defined as "a card, plate or other single credit device that may be used from time to time to obtain credit." Applies to every "public or private university, college, technical college or community college located in New Jersey."
- **18A:3C-2** — an issuer must **annually register its request to solicit** with an appropriate official of the institution *before* soliciting, disclosing its principal place of business plus whatever else the institution demands.
- **18A:3C-3** — a registered issuer that solicits must **provide a one-time, on-campus program of education on responsible credit use**, covering interest charges, introductory-to-ongoing rate shifts, minimum-payment payoff timelines, fees and grace periods, and prudent-use guidance.
**Effect:** any crypto project marketing a credit-linked card product in NJ must pre-register annually *and* deliver a financial-literacy program. That is weeks of lead time, not days.

### C. Federal — applies in all nine states: **no swag for card sign-ups**
**12 CFR 1026.57(c).** Card issuers may not offer college students **tangible items** — the rule names gift cards and t-shirts — to induce credit card applications **on campus, within 1,000 feet of campus, or at events sponsored by or related to the institution.** Non-physical inducements (discounts, rewards points, promotional credit terms) fall **outside** the prohibition. Institutions must also publicly disclose, unredacted, any card-marketing agreement.
**Effect:** the standard crypto-booth playbook — free merch for a sign-up — is federally barred wherever the product is a credit card. Swag for a *non-credit* sign-up is not reached by this section.

### D. Campus-level credit-card rule at the #1 pick
UConn's own U-Guide: credit-card **"Vending is limited to the Student Union Building,"** issuers **"must register and distribute credit management education materials,"** and the organization name must be clearly visible. This exists independently of any state statute and confirms UConn actively administers a campus credit-card regime.

### E. Connecticut campus credit-card legislation — **UNVERIFIED**
A 2002 CT OLR report (2002-R-0100) and a 2009 bill (SB-73, "An Act Concerning the Solicitation of Credit Cards to College Students") both exist, but **cga.ct.gov was unreachable (robots.txt fetch timeouts)**, so I could **not** verify whether CT enacted a statute. Treat CT as *possibly* having one. URLs for follow-up:
- https://www.cga.ct.gov/2002/rpt/2002-R-0100.htm
- https://www.cga.ct.gov/2009/jfr/s/2009SB-00073-R00HED-JFR.htm

### F. States where NO campus credit-card statute was found
**MA, MD, RI, DE, NH, VT, ME** — no such statute surfaced. State the negative carefully: this is *not found in these searches*, not *proven absent*. The known comparator statutes named in the brief (MN § 135A.145, PA 24 P.S. § 23-2302-A, WV § 18B-14-10) have **no counterpart** that surfaced in any of these seven.

### G. Money transmission generally
CT is confirmed above. **NJ and MA** operate money-transmission regimes, but I did **not** verify their specific treatment of virtual currency in this sweep — **UNVERIFIED**. No BitLicense/DACPA-style outright bar on unregistered activity was found in any of the nine states. Reference URL not yet read: https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/

### H. Institutional flat bans (not statutes, but equally fatal)
- **UMass Amherst** — non-university commercial promotion barred at tables outright.
- **Northeastern** — "Northeastern is not a marketplace"; all outside sales/solicitation prohibited absent written permission.
- **Harvard** — non-Harvard co-sponsorship generally not permitted; commercial advertisement/give-away prohibited without advance authorization.
- **U. of Delaware** — external groups cannot self-serve; a college, division, department or recognized student org must sponsor, and "use of University facilities solely for fund-raising purposes shall be limited."
