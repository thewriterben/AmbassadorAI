## STATEWIDE LEGAL FINDING — THE FORUM ACT DOES NOT REACH COMMERCIAL SPEECH

This is the single most valuable thing established for Ohio, and it is established on the
statutory text itself, not on commentary.

Ohio's campus free-speech statute is the **FORUM Act (Forming Open and Robust University Minds),
S.B. 40 of the 133rd General Assembly**, codified at **Ohio Rev. Code §§ 3345.0211 – 3345.0215**.
Ohio University's compliance report page is literally titled `ohio.edu/sb-40-forum-act-report`,
confirming the S.B. 40 / FORUM Act identification on a live university page.

The statute has three operative pieces, and each of them excludes DGD.

**1. § 3345.0212(A) protects only NONCOMMERCIAL activity.** Verbatim:

> "Except as permitted by this section and sections 3345.0213 and 3345.12 ... no state
> institution of higher education ... shall prohibit any individual from engaging in
> **noncommercial expressive activity** on campus, so long as the individual's conduct is
> lawful and does not materially and substantially disrupt the functioning of the institution."
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3345.0212

**2. § 3345.0215(A) defines "free speech" to expressly EXCLUDE commercial promotion.** This is
the decisive sentence and the one an ambassador should be able to recite:

> "'Free speech' means speech, expression, or assemblies protected by the First Amendment to the
> United States Constitution or Article I, Sections 3 and 11 of the Ohio Constitution, verbal or
> written, including, but not limited to, all forms of peaceful assembly, protests,
> demonstrations, rallies, vigils, marches, public speaking, distribution of printed materials,
> carrying signs, displays, or circulating petitions. **'Free speech' does not include the
> promotion, sale, or distribution of any product or service.**"
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3345.0215

That final sentence is the whole ballgame. Promoting a product or service is, by statutory
definition, not "free speech" for purposes of Ohio's campus free-speech regime. A DGD table
promoting a cryptocurrency is promotion of a product or service. The FORUM Act's protections
simply do not attach to it.

**3. § 3345.0213 makes outdoor areas public forums FOR THE CAMPUS COMMUNITY — a defined class
DGD is not in.** Verbatim:

> "(A)(1) Outdoor areas of campuses of state institutions of higher education are public forums
> **for campus communities**.
> (A)(2) State institutions of higher education shall not create 'free speech zones' or designate
> other outdoor areas of campuses where expressive activities are prohibited.
> (A)(3) [institutions may enforce] reasonable time, place, and manner restrictions [that are]
> viewpoint- and content-neutral [and] provide for ample alternative means for expressive
> activities [and] allow for members of a campus community to spontaneously and contemporaneously
> assemble and distribute literature.
> (B) Nothing in this section shall be interpreted as limiting the right of student expression
> elsewhere on campus."
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3345.0213

And "campus community" is defined at **§ 3345.0211**:

> "'Campus community' means **students, student groups, faculty, staff, and employees of a state
> institution of higher education and their invited guests**."
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3345.0211

The same section defines "expressive activities" as "any lawful verbal, written, audiovisual, or
electronic means by which individuals may communicate ideas, including all forms of peaceful
assembly, protests, speeches, distribution of literature, carrying and displaying signs, and
circulating petitions." Note that this definition, standing alone, contains no commercial
carve-out — which is exactly why the § 3345.0215 exclusion and the § 3345.0212 "noncommercial"
qualifier matter so much. An ambassador who reads only § 3345.0211 will over-claim.

**The single doorway the statute does leave open** is the phrase "and their invited guests."
A DGD representative who is genuinely invited by a student organization, a faculty member or a
department is, at that moment, a member of the campus community for forum purposes. That is a
real and usable path — but it is an invitation-based path that runs through a person, not a
right that DGD can assert on its own. And even then, the activity has to be noncommercial for
§ 3345.0212 to protect it, which a sales table is not.

**§ 3345.0214** requires each institution to file a compliance report with the governor, house
speaker and senate president within 180 days, publish it within three clicks of the home page,
word-searchable and without registration, and to file a supplementary report within thirty days
of any suit over expression rights. These reports are useful reconnaissance — Ohio University's
is at https://www.ohio.edu/sb-40-forum-act-report and states in terms that the university
"permits noncommercial expressive activity," which is the university's own restatement of the
commercial exclusion.

**Bottom line for ambassadors: do NOT cite the FORUM Act as a right to table in Ohio.** It is a
statute for student advocates and invited speakers. Every fee, permit, sponsorship requirement
and commercial-activity ban catalogued below is lawful under it. Citing it at a table will get
DGD corrected by a staff member who has read it, and will cost the relationship.

Note also that the FORUM Act binds only **state institutions of higher education**. Of the ten
campuses here, nine are public and bound; **Case Western Reserve University is private and is
not bound at all**, and has no public-forum obligation of any kind.

## STATEWIDE REGULATORY FINDING — OHIO MONEY TRANSMISSION AND VIRTUAL CURRENCY

The Ohio Division of Financial Institutions (DFI), within the Ohio Department of Commerce, takes
the position that crypto businesses generally need an Ohio money transmitter licence. From the
Division's own money-transmitter resources page:

> "If you buy or sell cryptocurrency as a business in Ohio, you generally must hold an Ohio money
> transmitter license under the Ohio Money Transmitters Act (OMTA)."
> — https://com.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/com/divisions-and-programs/financial-institutions/money-transmitters/guides-and-resources/money-transmitter-resources

The statute is **Ohio Revised Code Chapter 1315**, and the Division points specifically to
**R.C. 1315.01(G)** as the provision under which virtual currency is treated as money. The
general licensing trigger, from the Division's money-transmitter landing page, is that "a person
must hold a money transmitter license if the person receives money or its equivalent for
transmission from someone located in Ohio" — the phrase "or its equivalent" is what carries
virtual currency into scope.

The Division also issued **Interpretive Guidance 2022-01, effective August 31, 2022**, which
replaced prior guidance and requires cryptocurrency kiosk and BTM (bitcoin teller machine)
operators either to obtain a licence or to obtain a written determination from the Division
before serving Ohio customers. The Division publishes a "List of Cryptocurrency Kiosks/ATM
Companies."

**DFI main line: (614) 728-8400** · web.dfi@com.ohio.gov · Ohio Department of Commerce, 77 South
High Street, 23rd Floor, Columbus, OH 43215-6133. Divisional directory:
https://com.ohio.gov/wps/portal/gov/com/about-us/contact-us/commerce-divisional-directory

**Operational consequence for the tour.** Nothing at a DGD table should look like receiving
money or its equivalent for transmission. Handing out literature, explaining the project and
directing people to a website is not money transmission. Taking custody of funds, running an
on-the-spot exchange, or operating any kind of kiosk at a table is exactly the conduct the OMTA
reaches, and doing it unlicensed on a state university campus is the worst possible venue for
it. Also note the interaction with campus policy: several Ohio campuses (Miami, Ohio State,
BGSU) draw their line at *sales*, so a table that stays on the education side of the line is
simultaneously safer under Chapter 1315 and easier to defend under campus rules.

**On Ohio's former bitcoin tax-payment programme (OhioCrypto.com):** Ohio launched a programme
in late 2018 allowing businesses to pay certain state taxes in bitcoin through the Treasurer's
office, and it was suspended less than a year later. **I could not re-verify this on a live page
in this session** — the search tooling was exhausted and no Treasurer or Commerce page reachable
by direct URL confirmed it. Treat it as background only. It is in any event **not relevant to
the current licensing position**, which is the OMTA/Chapter 1315 posture set out above, and it
should not appear in DGD materials as evidence that Ohio is crypto-friendly. Marked UNVERIFIED.

## GEOGRAPHY — THREE CLUSTERS, ONE OF THEM UNUSUALLY DENSE

Ohio splits cleanly into three driving clusters, and this should shape the itinerary more than
any single campus's merits.

**Northeast Ohio is the densest cluster in the state.** Kent State (Kent), the University of
Akron (Akron), Case Western Reserve (Cleveland) and Cleveland State (Cleveland) all sit within
roughly 45 minutes' drive of one another. Kent to Akron is about 15 miles; Akron to Cleveland
about 40; Case Western and Cleveland State are roughly four miles apart within Cleveland itself
and can be walked between at a stretch. Four of the ten campuses in this packet are reachable
from a single hotel base. If an ambassador has one productive week in Ohio, it belongs here.

**Columbus is a cluster of one — but that one is enormous.** Ohio State's Columbus campus is one
of the largest single campuses in the United States by enrolment, and there is no second stop
within an hour that is worth a day.

**Cincinnati is the southwest cluster.** The University of Cincinnati sits in the city; Miami
University at Oxford is about 40 miles northwest of Cincinnati, which makes Cincinnati + Oxford
a comfortable pair on consecutive days.

**Two campuses sit outside all three clusters:** Ohio University at Athens is in the southeast
hills, roughly 75 miles southeast of Columbus and genuinely isolated — it is a dedicated trip,
not an add-on. The University of Toledo is in the far northwest, about two hours from both
Columbus and Cleveland, and pairs naturally with Bowling Green, which is only about 20 miles
south of Toledo. **Toledo + BGSU is the state's second-best pairing after the northeast four.**

## C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY

**Ohio State's operative rules are in the Ohio Administrative Code**, because OSU's board rules
are codified there as agency 3335. This is the cleanest, most quotable authority of any campus
in this packet, and an ambassador should carry both rules.

**Rule 3335-13-01, "Drives and canvassing" (effective July 2, 2014)** — verbatim:

> **"(A) CANVASSING OR SOLICITATION FOR FUNDS, SALES, OR SUBSCRIPTIONS ARE PROHIBITED ON
> UNIVERSITY CAMPUSES OR IN UNIVERSITY BUILDINGS UNLESS WRITTEN PERMISSION THEREFOR SHALL FIRST
> HAVE BEEN GRANTED BY THE OFFICE OF THE UNIVERSITY REGISTRAR ...**
> **(B) The sale of merchandise of any kind whatsoever, or publications or services upon
> university property, other than by the regularly authorized stores, restaurants, departments or
> divisions of the university, is likewise prohibited except upon written permission of the
> office of the university registrar ...**
> **(C) ANY PERSONS VIOLATING THIS RULE SHALL BE SUBJECT, UPON PROPER NOTICE, TO EVICTION FROM
> UNIVERSITY PROPERTY OR ARREST."**
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3335-13-01

Note the word "subscriptions" in (A). A wallet sign-up sheet or a mailing-list clipboard is
squarely within it. Note also that (C) contemplates **arrest**, not merely a request to leave —
this is the sharpest enforcement language in the Ohio set.

**Rule 3335-13-03, "University facilities" (effective October 16, 2023)** — the sponsorship rule,
verbatim:

> **"(A) University facilities may be scheduled and used only if the meeting is sponsored by at
> least one of the following: the university; an administrative unit of the university; the
> president or a member of their staff; a member of the university faculty; a club or committee
> of faculty members, staff members, or their spouses; or a recognized student organization."**
> "(B) The office of the university registrar shall be responsible for the scheduling of and
> shall have the necessary authority to schedule all university facilities." All meeting requests
> must obtain prior permission and indicate the nature of the meeting; **guest-speaker events
> require submission at least two weeks beforehand**, waivable if circumstances warrant.
> "(C) 'Meeting' [means] any meeting using university facilities to which the faculty, staff,
> students or public is invited, except regularly scheduled university courses, conferences
> approved under university procedures, and meetings of state, regional, national or
> international organizations approved under university procedures."
> "(D) The responsibility and authority of the office of the university registrar under this rule
> may be delegated by it to a member of the faculty or of the staff of this university."
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3335-13-03

**Does sponsorship cure the problem at OSU? Partly, and only for the facility.** 3335-13-03
makes faculty sponsorship or RSO sponsorship a valid basis for *scheduling a facility* — unlike
Oklahoma, OSU does not prohibit sponsorship of outside groups. **But 3335-13-01 is a separate
prohibition on solicitation and sales that sponsorship does not touch**; that one needs written
permission from the Registrar regardless of who sponsors the room. The two rules stack. An
ambassador who secures a faculty sponsor has solved the venue problem and not the sales problem.

**No anti-"fronting" language was found in either rule.** That is a genuine finding, not an
omission — OSU's rules do not contain the "fronting is prohibited" clause that Oklahoma and
several other states use. The functional equivalent is that solicitation needs Registrar
permission independent of the sponsor.

## C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY

UC has the most explicit commercial-solicitation rule in the state, and it is codified.

**Rule 3361:10-51-01, "Sales and solicitation" (effective April 1, 2019)** — verbatim, the
operative provisions:

> **(A)(1) Definitions.** "'Sales' [means] the activity of exchanging a product or service in
> return for money, goods, or other services." "'Solicitation' [means] the activity or process of
> seeking to obtain the support of an individual for a cause, movement, doctrine, **or commercial
> product** through persuasion or formal application."
>
> **(A)(2) Sales by non-university organizations. "THERE SHALL BE NO SALES AND SOLICITATIONS
> CONDUCTED ON UNIVERSITY PROPERTY, OUTSIDE THE PARAMETERS OF THIS POLICY, EXCEPT BY VENDORS WITH
> WHOM THE UNIVERSITY HAS CONTRACTED FOR THE SALE OF GOODS AND SERVICES."**
>
> **(A)(3)** "The sale of service or products on university property shall only be conducted by
> students, faculty, or staff of the university of Cincinnati **or by representatives of companies
> that have received approval from the required university office as outlined in paragraph (B)**."
>
> **(A)(4)** "Sales may not normally be conducted in competition with the products and services
> normally provided by the bookstores, Tangeman university center, or other university facilities."
>
> **(A)(6) Solicitation.** "Solicitation on university property may only be conducted by students,
> faculty, or staff of the university of Cincinnati **or by representatives of companies that have
> received approval from the required university office**."
>
> **(A)(9) Contract approval.** "All contracts between the university (or its departments) and
> other parties shall be approved in the required fashion using the university's contract approval
> process (form A-910)."
>
> **(B)(1) Procedure — THE DOOR.** **"REQUESTS TO CONDUCT SALES OR SOLICITATION ON UNIVERSITY
> PROPERTY MUST BE APPROVED BY THE PURCHASING DEPARTMENT,** which may consult with both the
> person(s) responsible for the building, as outlined in the university of Cincinnati use
> facilities policy manual and, if required, the office of student organizations and activities
> (for student sponsored activity on the west campus)."
>
> (C)/(D) A standing "sales and solicitation committee" reviews the policy, with representatives
> from faculty senate, finance, the graduate student governance association, the provost's office,
> student affairs, student government and the libraries.
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3361:10-51-01

**This is an unusual and genuinely actionable finding.** At most campuses the approving office is
student affairs or a union manager. **At UC it is PURCHASING.** That reframes the ask entirely:
DGD is not asking permission to speak, it is applying to be an approved vendor. Note the phrasing
in (A)(3) and (A)(6) — "representatives of companies that have received approval" — the rule
expressly contemplates that a company's representatives can be approved.

**Use of Facilities Policy Manual (Board of Trustees, August 2025)** — the companion document.
Verbatim on who may use space:

> **"THE RENTAL OR USE OF UNIVERSITY FACILITIES IS RESTRICTED TO THE UNIVERSITY'S CURRENT
> STUDENTS, FACULTY, STAFF, AND REGISTERED STUDENT ORGANIZATIONS, AND THOSE PERSONS OR GROUPS
> THEY SPONSOR."**

**Sponsorship therefore CURES the access problem at UC** — but with an escort condition, verbatim:

> Students, RSOs, faculty and staff who sponsor external groups **"must accompany the sponsored
> person or group during the entire duration that the University facility is being used."**

Other operative terms from the manual, verbatim:
> "Services of facilities are not available for fundraising purposes if the funds are designed
> solely to enrich an individual or commercial sponsor."
> "Banners may not display advertising logos or commercial sponsorship, or off-campus vendors."
> On tabling: "When scheduling the event in individual colleges or facilities, the sponsor is
> referred to the appropriate facility office to obtain approval. If the facility office personnel
> determines the dissemination of information at the point desired interferes with traffic or
> internally planned programs, they may refuse the request or suggest a different location."
> "The sponsoring student, Registered Student Organization, faculty, or staff member is
> responsible for the actions of the person or group they sponsor."
> Fees: "Events in Tangeman University Center (TUC) and approved outdoor spaces do not have a fee
> associated with reservation of space for University Groups or Registered Student Organizations
> **unless the event charges admission, has conference/class fees, exhibit fees, or any other
> exchange of funds.**"
> A published external figure: non-UC groups may reserve the African-American Cultural and
> Resource Center "upon availability and must pay a **$500 reservation fee**."
> **INSURANCE (athletic facilities):** "The lessee should evidence **$2,000,000 in limit per
> occurrence in general liability**, which provides coverage for bodily injury and personal injury,
> and property damage," with the University "added as 'Additional Insureds'" and the certificate
> due **not less than 14 days before** the start of the lease term.
> **CANCELLATION:** "The Requestor is responsible for all costs the university has incurred for
> events that are reserved but subsequently cancelled. Cancellation fees apply, as stated in the
> Event Guide, when a cancellation occurs close to the event date." The Event Guide itself,
> published by Conference & Event Services, carries the current cancellation terms and was not
> retrievable.
> — https://uc.edu/content/dam/refresh/af-62/af-policies/use-of-facilities-manual.pdf

**No anti-fronting clause was found at UC.** The escort requirement is the functional control:

walking-and-talking; it does not support tabling. And it says nothing about the commercial
character of the activity, which remains governed by the FORUM Act's commercial exclusion and by
whatever Kent's own solicitation rules say.

## C. SOLICITATION / OUTSIDE-VENDOR POLICY

CSU's rules are codified as OAC agency **3344**, and the relevant chapter is **3344-90** — which,
despite being titled "Campus Visitors," contains the space rules.

Chapter 3344-90 contains exactly three rules:
- **3344-90-01** — University space
- **3344-90-02** — Use of space for non-academic purposes
- **3344-90-03** — Policy regarding "persona non grata" status for campus visitors

**Rule 3344-90-02, "Use of space for non-academic purposes," effective June 5, 2021** (prior
effective dates December 24, 1981 and July 7, 2014) — the operative provision, verbatim:

> **"UNIVERSITY SPACE NOT REQUIRED FOR ACADEMIC PURPOSES, MAY BE USED IN A MANNER, WHICH PROVIDES
> FOR REIMBURSEMENT TO THE UNIVERSITY FOR ALL DIRECT COSTS OF THE USE, AS WELL AS OTHER APPLICABLE
> FEES AS SET FORTH IN THE FACILITIES USE PROCEDURES."**
>
> The university president or designee must approve the facilities use procedures; those procedures
> must set out how facilities are requested, reserved and approved for non-academic purposes; and
> **non-CSU community members are permitted to use facilities under those procedures**.
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3344-90-02

**This is a cost-reimbursement model, and it is a real door.** CSU's codified rule contemplates
non-academic use by non-CSU parties on a full-direct-cost basis. It contains **no commercial
prohibition**, **no sponsorship requirement**, and **no tax-exempt requirement** — which
distinguishes it sharply from BGSU. What it does is delegate every operational detail (who
approves, what it costs, what insurance is needed) to a separate **"facilities use procedures"**
document. **That document was not located and is the key CSU gap.**

**Rule 3344-90-03, "persona non grata" — the enforcement backstop.** Verbatim:

> "'Visitor' means any person who is neither a student, as defined in the student code of conduct,
> nor an employee of the university."
> The rule provides "a means for regulating the behavior of all persons, except students and
> university employees, who are present on university property."
> "Behavior detrimental to the university community" includes disruption of programmes, violation
> of legitimate orders from university officials, continuing violations of university rules,
> document falsification, or misrepresentation to university offices.
> Designated visitors may receive **"persona non grata" status**, meaning they **"are not permitted
> to be present in any university locations."**
> — https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/chapter-3344-90

A DGD representative is, by this definition, a **"visitor."** The persona non grata mechanism is
the sanction for getting it wrong at CSU, and note that "misrepresentation to university offices"
is an enumerated ground — another reason not to route a booking through a student club under false
colours.

## 4. THE STATE CAMPUS FREE-SPEECH STATUTE

**Ohio has one, it is the FORUM Act (S.B. 40), and it does NOT reach commercial speech.** Full
analysis at the top of this document. The three sentences that decide it:

1. § 3345.0215(A): **"'Free speech' does not include the promotion, sale, or distribution of any
   product or service."**
2. § 3345.0212(A): protects only **"noncommercial expressive activity."**
3. § 3345.0213(A)(1) + § 3345.0211: outdoor areas are public forums **"for campus communities,"**
   defined as "students, student groups, faculty, staff, and employees ... **and their invited
   guests**."

It binds the nine public campuses in this packet. **It does not bind Case Western Reserve.** The
only usable path it offers DGD is the **"invited guest"** clause — which runs through a person who
invites you, not through a right you can assert.

**There is no single systemwide facilities rule in Ohio.** Every public university here sets its
own trustee-level policy, and the governing layer differs campus by campus:

- **Ohio State, Cincinnati, Kent State, BGSU, Cleveland State** — trustee rules **codified in the
  Ohio Administrative Code** (agencies 3335, 3361, 3342, 3341, 3344 respectively). These are the
  most quotable and the hardest to waive informally.
- **Ohio University, Toledo, Miami** — **university policy libraries** (numbered policies like
  01.044, 3364-30-01, and Armstrong Student Center policies) that are not all in the OAC.
- **Akron** — layer **unverified**; agency 3359 exists but the relevant chapters checked contain
  no facilities-use rule, so the operative document is probably a non-codified university policy.
- **Case Western** — **private institutional policy only**, no public-law layer at all.
