### C. Solicitation / Outside-Vendor Policy — THE BEST DOOR IN NEW MEXICO

NMSU splits expressive speech and commercial speech into two separate policies, and — critically — **its commercial policy expressly contemplates and permits sponsored outside vendors.**

**1. ARP 3.63 — "Freedom of Expression"** (revised **July 21, 2015**; replicated Oct 21, 2015)
- Applies to **"all people"** across NMSU system property — external parties get the same protection as university members in designated forums.
- Foundation: *"Free exchange of ideas is a reflection of the university's public land-grant heritage, interest in diverse points of view, and commitment to excellence."*
- **⚠️ CARVE-OUT THAT CONTROLS DGD:** *"Speech that proposes a commercial transaction"* is **excluded** from ARP 3.63 and is instead governed by **ARP 14.92 (Sales and Solicitation)**. False or misleading commercial speech receives no First Amendment protection.
- **Forum taxonomy (three types):** traditional public forums (parks, sidewalks, lobbies); limited public forums (event-specific areas, restrictions tied to purpose); non-public forums (offices, classrooms, archives). NMSU does **not** use fenced "free speech zones" — it uses forum classification.
- **Threshold trigger:** activities drawing **250+ participants require advance written notification.** Permits are required for anything closing roads/parking or denying property access.
- **Time/place/manner:** electronic amplification permitted *"8:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m., Sunday through Thursday; 8:00 a.m. to midnight Friday and Saturday"* in designated outdoor areas. Signage allowed 24 hours pre/post-event without permit.
- Space requests coordinated through the Office of Student Involvement and Leadership Programs.
- URL: https://arp.nmsu.edu/chapter-3/3-63.html

**2. ARP 14.92 — "Sales and Solicitation"** (effective **April 12, 2019**)
- **Definition, verbatim:** solicitation is *"The act of selling or encouraging the purchase of a product either directly or indirectly."* Note "or indirectly" — this is broad enough to capture promotional crypto outreach even without an on-site transaction.
- **Baseline:** commercial advertising and solicitation are **generally prohibited** on campus. Narrow exceptions: media advertising, donated physical property bearing donor names, and in-person solicitation **by direct invitation only**.
- **⚑ SPONSORSHIP IS THE CURE AT NMSU — the key differentiator from UNM.** Outside vendors may conduct sales on campus **only when "sponsored by a recognized campus organization or a University Department,"** with **prior written approval from the director of student involvement and leadership programs.**
- **Process:** student organizations complete an **Activity Registration Form**; departments submit written requests to the **vice president for student enrollment and success at least 2 weeks prior**.
- **Anti-fronting (weak form):** organizations *"incorporated outside the university which raise funds for a University Department"* must confer with the **vice president for university advancement** before fundraising. This is a fundraising-directed provision, not a general bar on a student org hosting an outside entity.
- **Not found in the retrieved text:** no fee schedule, no insurance requirement, no credit-card/payment-app language, no on-site contract restriction. **Mark these UNVERIFIED and confirm with SILP.**
- URL: https://arp.nmsu.edu/chapter-14/14-92.html

**Practical synthesis for NMSU:** This is the cleanest legitimate path in the state. Recruit **one** recognized student organization (the Financial Management Association is the obvious candidate), have them file an Activity Registration Form naming DGD as a sponsored outside vendor, and obtain written approval from the SILP director. Unlike UNM, this route is **expressly authorized rather than expressly penalized.** Build in ≥2 weeks lead time.

## (4) State Campus Free-Speech Statute — FINDING: NONE EXISTS

**New Mexico has no campus free-speech statute.** This is a negative finding, and it is a real one.

**Evidence:**
- **UNM's own dedicated free-speech portal** (`https://freespeech.unm.edu/policies-law/index.html`) maintains a "Policies & Law" page that enumerates the New Mexico statutes it considers applicable to campus expression. **Every one is a criminal-code provision, not a campus free-expression act:** §30-3A-2 Harassment; §30-8-1 Public Nuisance; §30-11-1(C) Libel; §30-14-1 Criminal Trespass; §30-14-4(2) Wrongful Use of Public Property; §30-18B Hate Crimes; §30-20-1 Disorderly Conduct; §30-20-3 Unlawful Assembly; §30-20-13(C & D) Interference with Staff, Officials, or Public. **These constrain speakers; none confers campus forum rights.** If a campus free-speech act existed, UNM's own free-speech office would list it here.
- **NMSU's ARP 3.63 (Freedom of Expression)** grounds itself in the **First Amendment and the New Mexico Constitution** — not in any state statute. A land-grant university writing a dedicated expression policy would cite a controlling statute if one existed.
- Searches of NM legislative sources, the Goldwater Institute's model-legislation adoption record, and the First Amendment Encyclopedia's campus free-speech-law survey produced no New Mexico enactment. The Encyclopedia notes *"at least 23 states as of 2024"* adopted campus free speech protection acts but **does not enumerate them**, so this cannot be closed from that source alone.

**Confidence: high, but formally UNVERIFIED.** My web-search budget was exhausted before an exhaustive nmlegis.gov bill search could be completed. **Verify at https://www.nmlegis.gov/Legislation/Bill_Finder before relying on it in writing.**

**Why it matters operationally:** several western states (Arizona, Colorado, Utah, Arkansas among them) passed FORUM-Act-style statutes between 2018 and 2025 that convert public-campus outdoor areas into statutorily protected public forums, ban free-speech zones, and create a private right of action. **In New Mexico, none of that applies.** The ambassador has **no statutory lever** — the only constraints on these six public institutions are (a) baseline First Amendment public-forum doctrine and (b) each institution's own policy. Where the policy is unpublished (NMT, ENMU, CNM) or grants unconstrained discretion (NMHU), there is no statute to appeal to. Argue constitutional doctrine or negotiate; do not cite a New Mexico campus speech law, because there isn't one.
