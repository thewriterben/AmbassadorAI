Fast-Start Shortlist — campuses with a crypto club AND an open door

72 campuses across 27 states — 24 fully researched, plus a focused club-and-access sweep of 26 more · compiled August 12, 2026

Read this before you build the booth

You asked for campuses where an ambassador can direct interested students to downloading a wallet and validating. Those are two different acts and the law treats them completely differently. Splitting them is what makes this shortlist usable rather than a liability:

GENERALLY FINE

Install the wallet at the table

Installing a free app is not itself a regulated activity, and no campus rule found across all 50 states prohibits a student downloading software on their own phone. Demo it, walk them through it, answer questions. This is the part of your ask that works almost everywhere.

THIS IS THE REGULATED STEP

Create the account and issue the $21 credit at the table

Account creation plus credit issuance plus referral accrual is what Illinois and New York regulate, what Weber State means by "sign a contract for services," what Colorado State means by "financial transactions," and what Montana State means by "monetary exchange." It is also the step that pattern-matches to the CARD Act conduct every dean of students already knows about.

THE VERSION THAT SHIPS

Complete validation later, off campus, on their own device

Same student, same outcome, one hour later. Capture an email or let them scan a code they open at home. This single change makes the identical booth compliant at every campus researched — and it removes the argument an administrator would otherwise use to revoke access mid-tour.

The states and campuses below have written rules that specifically reach the second act. This is not a general caution — each one is a real sentence in a real policy or statute found during this research. Four states now have campus credit-card-marketing statutes (MN, PA, WV, NJ) and a federal rule applies everywhere, so treat "no swag for signups" as a national default rather than a local quirk.

STATEWIDE BAR — RESOLVE BEFORE ANY ILLINOIS TABLE

Illinois

Digital Assets and Consumer Protection Act, 205 ILCS 731 § 15-5 (eff. Aug 18, 2025): a person "shall not engage in digital asset business activity, OR HOLD ITSELF OUT AS BEING ABLE TO, with a resident unless registered" with IDFPR. A table whose stated purpose is signing students up is holding itself out. This is a registration question for counsel, not a campus question.

STATEWIDE BAR — RESOLVE BEFORE ANY NEW YORK TABLE

New York

23 NYCRR Part 200 (BitLicense). § 200.2(q)(5) puts ISSUING a virtual currency inside "Virtual Currency Business Activity" whenever it involves "New York or a New York Resident"; § 200.3 separately bars operating "through an agent or agency arrangement when the agent is not a Licensee" — which reads directly onto a campus ambassador. No student or promotional carve-out exists.

CAMPUS BAN — Colorado State University

Colorado

Lory Student Center: "No financial transactions (including requesting credit card information, Venmo or similar app info, etc) are permitted on the Plaza." Written broadly enough to reach wallet onboarding.

CAMPUS BAN — Weber State University

Utah

"Vendors may not have students sign any kind of contract for services on site; this must take place at the Vendor's place of business." Accepting terms of service at the table is signing.

CAMPUS BAN — Montana State University

Montana

Catapalooza: "Vendors are prohibited from seeking a monetary exchange for products, services, or donations." Contacts collected must be re-confirmed after the event before any transaction, and no mailing-list building without specific consent.

STATE STATUTE — reaches public AND private institutions

Minnesota

Minn. Stat. § 135A.145 bars every postsecondary institution "including its agents, employees, STUDENT OR ALUMNI ORGANIZATIONS, or affiliates" from agreements to market credit cards to undergraduates. It is why St. Cloud State bans "credit card or debit card sign-ups" and Augsburg bans "credit card promotions" — and it is the first statute a campus lawyer reaches for.

STATE STATUTE

Pennsylvania

24 P.S. § 23-2302-A requires every Pennsylvania institution to regulate credit-card marketing and permits an outright ban. CMU, West Chester and CCP all ban it; CMU's reaches "any approved tabling or university event."

12 C.F.R. § 1026.57(c) — THE SWAG BOWL RULE

FEDERAL — all 50 states

Regulation Z: no card issuer or creditor may offer a college student "any tangible item to induce such student to apply for or open an open-end consumer credit plan" ON campus, NEAR campus (commentary: within 1,000 FEET), or at an institution-related event. It reaches credit products specifically — but merch-for-signup at a folding table is the exact conduct it was written to stop, and it is the pattern administrators recognise on sight.

STATE STATUTE — N.J.S.A. 18A:3C-1 to -3

New Jersey

"Solicit" means ANY on-campus activity intended in whole or part to encourage a student to apply for a credit card, and "credit card" is defined broadly as any "card, plate or other single credit device." Issuers must register ANNUALLY with the institution before soliciting and deliver a one-time on-campus responsible-credit education programme. That is weeks of lead time, not days.

STATE STATUTE — W. Va. Code § 18B-14-10

West Virginia

Boards must REGISTER on-campus credit-card marketers, confine them to president-designated sites, PROHIBIT TANGIBLE GIFTS in exchange for completing an application, and require every application to carry a credit-card debt education brochure. Written around credit cards, so a token booth is not squarely inside it — but swag-for-signup is exactly the behaviour it flags, and the registration machinery is what a WVU administrator will reach for.

MONEY TRANSMISSION REACHES ADVERTISING

Connecticut

C.G.S. §§ 36a-595 to 36a-612. The CT Department of Banking lists licensure triggers as holding customer currency, transmitting it, AND "ADVERTISING MONEY TRANSMISSION SERVICES." No exemption is discussed for promotional or educational activity. This directly qualifies UConn — the strongest Northeastern pick — so keep the Connecticut table strictly educational.

VIRTUAL CURRENCY BUSINESS ACT

Louisiana

NMLS licence for exchanging, transferring or storing virtual currency; $35k registration tier; exceptions include personal and academic use. Marketing alone is not enumerated — but a wallet, fund movement or on-site exchange would be. OFI also maintains older money-transmitter guidance, so two frameworks may be asserted at once.

CAMPUS BAN — Missouri S&T

Missouri

"Credit card, telephone card, or other financial services vendors are not allowed at the Havener Center or on the Missouri S&T campus," backed by an anti-fronting rule that closes the club route.
