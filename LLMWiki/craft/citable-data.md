# Citable Data — current macro figures

**One canonical place for the outside-world numbers ambassadors quote.** Before this page
existed, figures like "CPI is 4.2%" were scraped out of dated `daily/` drops, which are archives
and go stale silently — a July script was citing a May print two releases out of date.

> **Never quote a figure from a `daily/` or `trends/` file.** Those are dated snapshots. Come here,
> check the date at the top, and if it's more than a month old, re-verify at the source before you
> record anything.

**Purchasing power:** at 3.5% inflation, a dollar held for a year buys about **3.4% less**
(1 − 1/1.035). Quote it that way — "3.5% inflation" and "3.4% purchasing-power loss" are the same
fact stated two ways, not two separate findings.

## ⚠️ The narrative changed — read this before you script

**Inflation fell.** June's −0.4% monthly was the **largest one-month decline since April 2020**.
Core was flat. Shelter posted its smallest monthly rise since January 2021.

A video opening "inflation is destroying your savings right now" is, this month, **out of step
with the data**, and a sceptical viewer with ten seconds and a search engine will catch it. That
costs you more credibility than the hook wins.

**The honest angle is stronger anyway:**

- **One good month doesn't undo cumulative erosion.** Prices are still 3.5% higher than a year ago,
  on top of every prior year. That is the actual sound-money argument, and it doesn't depend on
  which way this month's print went.
- **The volatility is the point.** Headline swung from +0.5% to −0.4% in a single month, driven
  almost entirely by energy (−5.7% on the month, but still **+15.7% over the year**). A measure
  that moves that much month to month is a poor thing to plan a life around — which is an argument
  about *predictability*, exactly what DGD's design claims to address.
- **Headline vs core is a genuinely useful explainer.** 3.5% headline against 2.6% core, with the
  gap driven by energy, is a clean teaching moment about what "core" strips out and why your
  petrol bill doesn't match the number on the news.

**Do not** frame a falling CPI as bad news, or imply the reprieve is temporary so people should act
now. Both are forecasting, and the second is solicitation. Report the number, explain the
mechanism, stop.

## Rules for citing any outside figure

1. **Date it on screen.** "June 2026" or "as of July 2026" in the caption or on-screen text.
2. **Name the source.** BLS, not "studies show."
3. **Re-verify before recording** if this page is over a month old. Releases land monthly.
4. **Never round in your favour.** 3.5% is 3.5%, not "nearly 4%."
5. **A figure is not an argument.** Inflation data explains a *mechanism*; it never implies anyone
   should acquire anything. See [do & don't language](../compliance/do-and-dont-language.md).
